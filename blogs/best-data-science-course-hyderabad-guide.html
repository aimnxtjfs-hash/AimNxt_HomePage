That is the real problem with choosing a data science course in Hyderabad. There is no shortage of options and no shortage of five-star badges. What is missing is the handful of facts you would need to compare two of them honestly. This guide supplies those facts, says where each one came from, and gives you a method you can apply to any institute on that page, including ours.

How do you choose the best data science course in Hyderabad?

Choose a data science course in Hyderabad on four published facts: the taught-hour count, the fee printed beside those hours, who actually awards the certificate, and whether the syllabus reaches deep learning and large language models. Of the nine institutes ranking for this phrase on 18 October 2026, not one publishes all four.

Everything else on those pages is decoration. A 4.9-star widget counting 31,245 reviews site-wide tells you nothing about the batch you would sit in. A list of hiring-partner logos tells you nothing about whether those companies interviewed anybody from your course. The four facts above are checkable before you pay, and they are what the rest of this page is about.

How much does a data science course cost in Hyderabad?

Google's own AI Overview for this query puts a standard Hyderabad data science training programme between Rs 45,000 and Rs 1,20,000, falling to Rs 25,000 for basic online certifications and rising past Rs 1,50,000 for university-backed postgraduate programmes. Codegnan publishes Rs 65,000, discounted from Rs 1,00,000, read on 18 October 2026.

Those are third-party market figures and they move. The useful thing about them is not the midpoint but the width: the same phrase, in the same city, covers a Rs 25,000 recorded-video certificate and a Rs 1,50,000 university programme. Nobody choosing between them is comparing like with like, and a headline discount of Rs 35,000 means nothing until you know how many hours sit behind it.

AimNxt does not publish fees in its blog posts and this page is no exception. What a trainer will do in a Free Live Demo Session is open the syllabus, tell you the batch length in hours, and let you do the arithmetic below on us as readily as on anyone else.

Why does the taught-hour count matter more than the number of months?

A month means nothing without an hour count: StarAgile publishes 148 hours of live instructor-led training inside a six-month course, while Innomatics publishes 200-plus hours with no duration at all. Neither prints a fee beside its hours, so on 18 October 2026 neither cost per taught hour could be calculated.

Months measure the calendar. Hours measure the teaching. A six-month course at two hours a week is 48 hours; a four-month course at ten hours a week is 160. Both are advertised the same way, and the second one is three times the training. Codegnan's page manages to describe the same programme as a one-month intensive, a six-month programme and a hundred days in three different places, which is a fair illustration of how little the month figure is doing.

So ask for the hour count, then divide. A course charging Rs 60,000 for 150 live hours is Rs 400 an hour. The same fee for 40 hours is Rs 1,500 an hour. That single number does more to separate Hyderabad institutes than any badge on the page, and almost nobody makes it easy to work out.

Whose certificate do you actually receive?

Most Hyderabad institutes front a certificate somebody else awards: NASSCOM FutureSkills Prime, TIH at IIT Bombay, IIT Madras Pravartak, SUNY or Microsoft. FutureSkills Prime is a joint nasscom and MeitY initiative, and its certification is earned by passing its own assessments, not by attending a partner institute's classes.

This matters because the wording on a course page is often doing quiet work. “NASSCOM FutureSkills Prime certified course” means the programme is aligned to that scheme, whose content is mapped to National Occupational Standards and the National Skills Qualification Framework; the certificate still comes from the scheme once you clear its assessment. Innomatics is explicit that the TIH at IIT Bombay certification applies to “eligible learners” against criteria, which is honest, and worth reading before you assume you are one of them.

AimNxt's position on this is deliberately plain. You receive an AimNxt certificate of completion. Cisco, EC-Council, CompTIA, Microsoft and the IIT-linked foundations are the certification bodies for their own credentials; AimNxt is an independent training provider that prepares you for them and does not administer anybody else's examination. If a Hyderabad institute implies the institute itself awards an IIT or a NASSCOM credential, ask which body signs it and what the assessment is.

Can you learn data science in 3 months?

Three months is enough to become employable in Python, SQL, statistics and classical machine learning, but not enough to also cover deep learning, computer vision, NLP and large language models properly. The AimNxt Data Science course content puts its classroom programme at four to six months for that reason.

The honest version of the three-month answer depends on what you want at the end of it. If you want a first job in a Hyderabad reporting or analyst role, three focused months on blocks 1 to 3 plus one reviewed project is a realistic plan, and the job data further down says that is the likelier first title anyway. If you want to be read for roles with GenAI, LLM or RAG in the title, that is blocks 7 to 10, and nobody teaches those properly in twelve weeks on top of the basics.

Be wary of the compression in the other direction too. A page advertising 300 hours inside a one-month intensive is asking for roughly ten hours a day, every day, which is possible for a full-time student and impossible for someone working.

Should you take a degree instead of a training programme?

Hyderabad offers both routes: IIIT Hyderabad runs a UGC-entitled online MSc in Data Science over two years for Rs 4,00,000 plus GST, against training programmes of three to six months at Rs 25,000 to Rs 1,50,000. The degree expects prior programming, calculus, linear algebra and probability.

They solve different problems. The MSc is 80 credits across 16 courses with proctored online examinations and an admissions committee, and it gives you a recognised postgraduate qualification. A training programme gives you working code, a portfolio and a route into interviews in months rather than years, with no entrance process. If you already have an engineering degree and need to get into the market this year, the training route is the sensible one. If you want the academic credential and can fund two years alongside work, the degree is real and local.

What the degree does usefully, for anyone comparing, is publish its prerequisites plainly. If calculus, linear algebra and probability are listed as an entry condition for a postgraduate programme, treat any three-month course promising to take you from zero to data scientist with appropriate suspicion.

Will your first job really be called data scientist?

Probably not. Naukri showed 169 Hyderabad data analyst vacancies open to zero years on 18 October 2026, against 64 data scientist vacancies on the same filter, roughly two and a half times as many doors. Most Hyderabad data careers start on a reporting or analyst title.

This is worth knowing before you choose a course, because it changes what you should optimise for. The first three blocks of any serious syllabus — Python and SQL, statistics, visualisation — are exactly what an analyst advert screens on, and they are the blocks you will use in month one of the job. Deep learning is what gets you promoted later, not hired now.

If the analyst route is where you are actually heading, the Data Analyst course in Hyderabad is the shorter, more direct version of the same first three blocks, and plenty of our students move across into the data science programme once they are earning. There is no shame in entering the market sideways; nearly everyone does.

Five questions to ask before you pay any Hyderabad institute

How many hours of live teaching, not recorded video? Ask for the number, then divide the fee by it. Anything above roughly Rs 1,000 per live hour deserves an explanation.
Who signs the certificate, and what must I pass to get it? If the answer is a third-party scheme, ask what the assessment is and whether you are eligible.
Does the syllabus reach deep learning, NLP and large language models? Ask to see the module list in writing, not a marketing summary.
What exactly is promised about jobs? “100% placement” and “placement assistance” are not the same sentence, and one of them is a claim nobody can keep.
Can I sit in a real class before paying? A free live demo with the trainer who would actually teach you answers more than any brochure.

Is data science a tough course?

Data science is hard in a specific way: the mathematics is undergraduate level, but you have to hold statistics, programming and business reasoning at once. The AimNxt Data Science course content asks for basic mathematics, statistics and programming concepts before you start, with Python familiarity recommended rather than required.

In practice, the people who struggle are rarely the ones who find the maths hard. They are the ones who treat it as a video course. Writing code that runs, breaking it, and being made to explain why a model behaves the way it does is the whole job, and it is why a reviewed capstone project is worth more at an interview than any certificate. If you are starting from a non-technical degree, expect the first two blocks to take real evening hours and plan for them honestly.

If you want the ground-level version before committing, read what data science actually is and how it differs from machine learning first. Both are short, and both will tell you fairly quickly whether this is the work you want to do.
