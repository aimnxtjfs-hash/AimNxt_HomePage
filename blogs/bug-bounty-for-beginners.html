We read the whole of Google’s first page for “bug bounty for beginners” on 2 October 2026, from India. There is no AI Overview. The top organic result is a personal Medium post whose own snippet says the author submitted 500 reports before earning a first bounty. Below it sit three Reddit threads. Below those, a video pack in which a creator states you should expect your first payout in 20 to 50 hours, averaging $100 to $500 per bug.

Both of those things are on the same screen, and they cannot both be true for the same person. One is a researcher describing what actually happened to him. The other is an estimate with nothing behind it. Google is showing you both because it genuinely does not know which to believe — and when we ran the India version of the query, it printed “Missing: India” against two of its own results, which is Google admitting there is almost nothing written for this reader.

So this guide is written for that reader. Not “what is a bug bounty” — you can get that anywhere. Whether it is legal for you to test anything under Indian law, what a first year honestly looks like, how much harder 2026 is than 2024, and whether any of it leads to a salary in Hyderabad. The technical sequence below follows the modules of the AimNxt VAPT & Ethical Hacking programme, because the skills that pay bounties and the skills that pass a penetration testing interview are the same skills in a different order.

What is bug bounty hunting, and can a beginner really do it?

Bug bounty hunting is being paid by a company to find and report security flaws in its systems under a published set of rules. Anyone can register on HackerOne or Bugcrowd today, with no degree and no certification. What stops beginners is not access — it is that finding a flaw nobody else has found takes months of practice.

The model is simple. A company publishes a policy: here are the systems you may test, here is what you may not do, here is what we pay for what severity. You find something, you write it up, a triager reproduces it, and if it is valid and in scope you are paid. No contract, no interview, no notice period. That openness is the appeal and it is also the problem, because every other beginner in the world has the same open door.

The useful way to think about it: a bug bounty program is not an employer and not a course. It is an exam with no syllabus, no pass mark, and thousands of other candidates sitting it at the same time on the same paper. Everything below is about making yourself one of the few who find something the others walked past.

Is bug bounty hunting legal in India?

Testing a system is legal in India only when the owner has authorised it in writing, and a published bug bounty policy is that written authorisation. Without it, unauthorised access is an offence. CERT-In’s disclosure policy states plainly that the discloser is responsible for any action performed in discovering the vulnerability.

That sentence is worth reading twice, because it is the whole safe-harbour question in one line. India’s Responsible Vulnerability Disclosure and Coordination Policy, published by CERT-In and read on 2 October 2026, will coordinate a fix for you — but it does not grant you permission to go looking. Permission comes from the target, and only from the target.

In practice that gives you three situations, and only one of them is safe:

A published program

The company has a policy page, a scope list and rules of engagement. You are authorised for exactly what that page says and nothing beyond it. This is the only situation in which you should be running a single test.

A disclosure policy with no bounty

A security.txt file or a “report a vulnerability” page, but no reward and often no explicit testing permission. Read it carefully. Many of these invite reports without authorising active testing.

Nothing published at all

No policy, no contact, no scope. Scanning it anyway is unauthorised access, whatever your intentions were. If you have already found something by accident, stop and report it rather than confirming it further.

The practical rule we teach

If you cannot point at a URL that gives you permission, you do not have permission. Screenshot the scope page with its date before you start, and keep it. Good intentions are not a defence under Indian law, and a bounty paid later does not retrospectively authorise testing done earlier.

Are there bug bounty programs in India?

Yes — India has both a government reporting channel and private company programs, though the government route pays nothing. CERT-In accepts vulnerability reports at vulnerability@cert-in.org.in, offers no reward of its own, and aims to resolve issues within 120 days of first contacting the vendor. Indian product companies run paid programs on the usual platforms.

The CERT-In policy is explicit about the money: it offers no direct reward, and says only that if the affected vendor wishes to reward the discloser in some way, CERT-In is willing to connect them. Its role is an introduction. That is genuinely useful when you have found something in a product with no security contact, and it is useless as an income plan.

For paid work, Indian targets appear on the same global platforms everyone else uses — fintech, SaaS and consumer app companies headquartered here run public and private programs on HackerOne, Bugcrowd and Intigriti. There is no India-only paid platform with enough programs to build a practice on, which is why the “best Indian bug bounty platform” question has no good answer and most articles answering it are guessing.

One detail nobody mentions: CERT-In also requires that the issue is reproducible on a currently supported version and has not already been disclosed, and asks for affected product, version, vendor, description, reproduction steps and supporting evidence. That is the same report structure a paid platform expects. If you can write a report CERT-In will accept, you can write one HackerOne will accept.

Before you test anything: the scope and authorisation check

Every legal bug bounty finding starts with four confirmations: a published policy exists, it authorises security testing, your exact target is named in scope, and your technique is permitted. Miss any one and you are not a researcher, you are an intruder. Use the checker below before you open Burp Suite.

This is the check we make students run in class before a single request leaves their machine. It takes about ninety seconds and it is the difference between a finding and a police complaint. Answer honestly — “probably fine” counts as no.

How much can a beginner realistically earn from bug bounty in India?

Most beginners earn nothing in their first six months, and the honest median first-year figure is closer to zero than to any monthly salary. The top organic result on Google for this topic is a researcher describing 500 submitted reports before a single bounty. Treat early earnings as training feedback, not income.

Nobody publishes a median. Platforms publish totals and record-breakers, because a hunter who earned a million dollars is a better advertisement than the distribution behind him. The distribution is extremely skewed: a small number of full-time hunters take most of the money, and the long tail earns occasional three-figure payouts for low-severity findings.

How hard is bug bounty hunting in 2026?

Harder than in any previous year, and HackerOne’s own data says so: 73% of its programs saw valid submissions fall and 50% saw critical submissions fall in 2025. Automated hackbots submitted over 1,100 findings, nearly half valid, and 78% of those valid hackbot findings were cross-site scripting.

Read that last number again, because it is the single most important fact for a beginner in 2026. Cross-site scripting is the bug every beginner guide tells you to start with. It is also the bug automation is clearing out first, at scale, before you wake up. The ninth edition of HackerOne’s Hacker-Powered Security Report, published 6 January 2026, is the source for all of these figures.

Two more findings from the same report change how you should plan. Valid AI-related findings rose 210% year on year and prompt injection rose 540%, with more than 1,121 programs now including AI in scope — a 270% increase. And 58% of surveyed researchers said they are actively upskilling in AI, while only 12% believe AI will replace them.

What this means for your first year

Do not plan to compete with a bot on volume. Plan to find the things a bot cannot judge: access control, business logic, and anything that requires understanding what the application is for. That is also, conveniently, what a penetration testing interview asks you about.

Which bug bounty platform is best for a beginner?

Start where programs are public and scopes are wide, because an invitation-only platform is closed to you until you have a track record. HackerOne and Bugcrowd carry the most public programs; Intigriti and YesWeHack have fewer hunters per program; Open Bug Bounty covers a very large set of sites without bounties.

The trade-off is always the same: the biggest platforms have the most targets and the most competition, and the smaller ones have less of both. For a first valid report, competition matters more than target count.

HackerOne

Largest, most crowded
  • Good forFinding a public program in almost any sector, and a well-run triage process that explains rejections.
  • Watch outPopular programs are picked over within hours of a scope change. Your signal score gates private invitations.

Bugcrowd

Large, good onboarding
  • Good forClear severity taxonomy, which teaches you how findings are graded before you argue about one.
  • Watch outSame crowding problem on the headline programs. Look past the first page of the program list.

Intigriti & YesWeHack

Smaller, less crowded
  • Good forEuropean programs with noticeably fewer hunters per target, which is exactly what a beginner needs.
  • Watch outFewer programs overall, so you may wait for a scope that fits what you can actually test.

Open Bug Bounty

No money, real practice
  • Good forA very wide target set and a low barrier, useful purely to practise the disclosure workflow end to end.
  • Watch outIt is coordinated disclosure, not a paid programme. Treat it as a rehearsal, not a source of income.

What skills do you need before your first hunt?

You need to read an HTTP request and understand what the application does with every value in it — that single ability is the gate. Add Linux comfort, basic HTML and JavaScript, and enough Python to script a repeated request. Nothing else on a typical beginner checklist matters until those four are real.

Notice what is not on that list. No certification. No degree. No Kali Linux ricing. No list of 40 tools. Beginners collect tools because tools are purchasable and understanding is not, and it is the single most common way to spend six months and learn nothing.

Read a request and response — headers, parameters, cookies, status codes, and what changes when you change one value.
Understand sessions and authorisation — who the server thinks you are, and how it decides what you may reach.
Linux command line — file permissions, processes, piping output, and reading a log without panicking.
Enough HTML and JavaScript — to see why a value rendered in a page becomes executable, and why cookies have flags.
One proxy, learned properly — Burp Suite Community is enough. Intercept, repeat, compare. That is 80% of the work.
Writing in plain English — underrated and decisive. A valid bug with an unclear report is a closed ticket.

How do you start bug bounty hunting as a beginner?

Learn web fundamentals first, practise on deliberately vulnerable applications, then pick one public program with a wide scope and stay on it for weeks. PortSwigger’s Web Security Academy is free and has 30 cross-site scripting labs and 16 SQL injection labs alone. Rotating targets every day is why most beginners find nothing.

1

Get the fundamentals, not the tools

HTTP, sessions, the same-origin policy, how a server handles input. Four to eight weeks if you are starting cold. You cannot shortcut this and every person who tries loses the time later.

2

Break things you are allowed to break

DVWA and bWAPP locally, plus the free labs on PortSwigger’s Web Security Academy. Finish the access control and authentication tracks before you touch a live target.

3

Read other people’s accepted reports

Disclosed reports on HackerOne are a free syllabus written by people who were paid. Read fifty before you write one. You will see the report format long before you see a bug.

4

Pick one public program and stay

Wide scope, responsive triage, not one of the famous names. Give it six weeks. Depth on one target beats a scan of twenty, and the hunters who find things are the ones who know a target well.

5

Map before you test

Enumerate subdomains, list endpoints, note every place the application takes input or makes an authorisation decision. Most findings come out of the map, not out of a payload list.

6

Report at minimum proof and stop

Prove the issue exists, screenshot it, write it up, submit. Do not pull records, do not pivot, do not go back for more. Over-testing after proof is the fastest way to lose safe harbour.

Which vulnerabilities should a beginner hunt first?

Access control flaws are the best beginner target in 2026, because they require judgement a scanner cannot supply and automation has not cleared them out. Broken Access Control is the number one category in OWASP Top 10:2025, with 40 CWEs under it. Cross-site scripting is the crowded opposite.

The reasoning is the hackbot data above. If 78% of valid automated findings are cross-site scripting, then the reflected XSS you are hunting on a public program has most likely already been reported by a machine while you were reading the scope page. Access control is different: deciding that user A should not be able to reach user B’s invoice needs someone who understands the business, and no scanner understands the business.

What makes a bug bounty report get accepted?

A report is accepted when a triager can reproduce the issue in under five minutes and see the business impact without asking you a question. That means exact steps, the raw request and response, a screenshot or short video, and one plain sentence on what an attacker gains.

Most rejected reports are not rejected because the bug was not real. They are rejected because the triager could not reproduce it, or because the writer described a technical oddity and never said what it lets an attacker do. “The server returns a stack trace” is an observation. “The stack trace exposes the database host and version, which narrows an attack to known CVEs for that version” is a finding.

The structure that works is the same one used in professional engagements, which is why it is a module and not an afterthought in the AimNxt syllabus. Our walkthrough of how to write a penetration testing report covers the full professional format; a bounty report is a compressed version of the same thing.

Numbered reproduction steps starting from a logged-out browser, not from your already-configured proxy.
The raw request and response as text, not a screenshot of text, so the triager can paste it straight into Repeater.
Impact in one sentence — what an attacker gets, whose data it is, and whether it scales to all users.
A remediation suggestion that is specific. “Validate input” is not one. “Enforce the ownership check server-side on this endpoint” is.

What mistakes waste a beginner’s first six months?

The expensive mistakes are running automated scanners at a target and submitting the output, chasing a new program every day, and reporting findings that carry no business impact. Each one burns reputation on platforms that rank you by signal. A low signal score closes private invitations before you ever get one.

The scanner mistake deserves its own warning, because it is also the one most likely to get you removed from a program. Many scopes explicitly forbid automated scanning or set a request rate. Pointing a noisy tool at a production host, then submitting its unverified output, fails on both counts at once: you broke the rules of engagement and you submitted something you cannot explain.

Scanner spraying

Unverified tool output, submitted in bulk. Rejected, reputation damaged, often a rules violation on top.

Target hopping

A new program every evening means you never learn any application well enough to see what is wrong with it.

Impactless findings

Missing headers, version banners, self-XSS. Technically true, closed as informative, and they cost you signal.

Tool collecting

Installing forty tools instead of learning one proxy properly. Our guide to the VAPT tools that matter is deliberately short for this reason.

Does bug bounty hunting get you a job in India?

Not directly — bug bounty is a skill tag on a penetration testing job, not a job title. Naukri showed 95 Hyderabad listings matching “bug bounty” on 2 October 2026, but only 7 sat in the IT and Information Security department against 62 in Engineering, Software and QA.

We read those listings. Filtered to zero years of experience, the whole of Hyderabad and Secunderabad returned five results, and exactly one was a genuine security role: a Security Analyst, Attack Surface Management position open to 0–4 years, tagging Burp Suite, OWASP and OSCP. The other four were a telecaller, a US tax associate, a business analyst and a graphic designer. That is what keyword matching does to a term that is not really a job title.

So the honest framing is this. Bug bounty is a very good way to build evidence that you can find real flaws in real systems, and that evidence is persuasive in an interview for a penetration testing or application security role. It is a poor plan for replacing a salary. The salary is paid for the same skills under a different name, and the roles that pay it ask for OSCP, Burp Suite and OWASP in their skill tags.
