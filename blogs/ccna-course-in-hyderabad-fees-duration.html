What Is a CCNA Course and What Does It Cover?

A CCNA course trains you to configure, operate, and troubleshoot enterprise networks, and prepares you for Cisco's CCNA 200-301 exam. The 2026 syllabus covers six domains: Network Fundamentals, Network Access, IP Connectivity, IP Services, Security Fundamentals, and Automation and Programmability. At AimNxt in KPHB, Hyderabad, CCNA is Module 1 of the Networking Ignite Programme.

CCNA — Cisco Certified Network Associate — is the entry point into professional networking, and Cisco's most widely recognised associate-level certification. The official CCNA 200-301 exam topics are published by Cisco, and any serious course should map to them exactly.

One thing worth saying early: you do not need to code, and you do not need an IT degree. CCNA suits B.Tech, BCA, and BSc graduates, but also career changers who have never touched a router. What it does demand is lab time. Networking is learned at the command line, not from slides.

How Long Does a CCNA Course in Hyderabad Take?

Most CCNA-only courses in Hyderabad run 6 to 8 weeks on weekday batches, or 8 to 10 weeks on weekends. AimNxt teaches CCNA as Module 1 of the Networking Ignite Programme (NIP), which runs 180 total hours including lab sessions over 3 to 4 months, followed by one year of LMS access and lab assistance.

Why the difference? A 6-week course covers the CCNA syllabus and stops. The NIP continues into CCNP ENCOR, firewalls, and enterprise wireless, because that combination is what Hyderabad employers actually interview for. If your goal is only the certification exam, a short course works. If your goal is the job, plan for the longer route.

What Is the Fee for a CCNA Course in Hyderabad?

CCNA training fees in Hyderabad vary widely between institutes because you are paying for very different things: lab hardware, batch size, trainer experience, and placement support. The Cisco CCNA 200-301 exam fee is separate from any training fee — USD 300 plus taxes, paid directly to Cisco through Pearson VUE (Cisco pricing, 2026).

We deliberately do not publish fee figures in this blog, and here is the honest reason: a single number tells you nothing. A cheap course with 40 students per batch and no physical equipment costs more in the end than a properly equipped one, because you sit through it and still cannot pass a practical interview round.

When you compare quotes, ask what the fee actually buys:

What Actually Drives a CCNA Course Fee

Physical lab hardware — real routers, switches, firewalls, and wireless controllers cost far more to run than a simulator licence. That cost shows up in the fee, and in your skills.
Batch size — 15 students per trainer costs more to deliver than 50. It also means your doubts get answered the same day.
Trainer experience — engineers with 10+ years of field experience charge more than fresh graduates reading slides.
What happens after class — LMS access, lab assistance, mock interviews, and resume support are either included or they are not. Ask.
The exam fee is never included by default — the CCNA 200-301 exam is Cisco's, booked via Pearson VUE, and billed separately. Confirm this with any institute.

What Does the CCNA Syllabus Include in 2026?

The CCNA 200-301 syllabus in 2026 covers six domains: Network Fundamentals, Network Access, IP Connectivity, IP Services, Security Fundamentals, and Automation and Programmability. AimNxt's CCNA module — Implementing and Administering Cisco Solutions — follows these six domains exactly, so classroom sessions, lab practice, and exam preparation stay aligned from the first week.

Network Fundamentals

How networks are built: switches, routers, cabling, TCP/IP, IPv4 and IPv6 addressing, and subnetting — the base everything else stands on.

Network Access

VLANs, trunking, EtherChannel, and wireless LAN access — how devices connect to the network and how you segment traffic.

IP Connectivity

Routing tables, static routing, and OSPF — how traffic finds its way between networks, and what you check when it does not.

IP Services

NAT, NTP, DHCP, DNS, SNMP, and QoS — the everyday services a working network engineer configures and troubleshoots.

Security Fundamentals

Access control lists, port security, VPN concepts, and wireless security — the baseline security every network job now expects.

Automation and Programmability

REST APIs, controller-based networking, and configuration management concepts — the newest domain, and the one interviewers increasingly probe.

This module prepares you for the Cisco Certified Network Associate (CCNA 200-301) exam. To be clear about what certificate you receive: Cisco is the certification body and conducts the exam; AimNxt is an independent training provider, and you receive an AimNxt certificate of completion for the course itself.

Why Do Real Cisco Labs Matter More Than Simulators?

Real Cisco labs let you rack, cable, configure, and break physical equipment the way you will in a production network — something a simulator can only partially reproduce. AimNxt's KPHB lab runs 3 routers, 3 L3 switches, 2 L2 switches, 3 next-generation firewalls, 2 wireless LAN controllers, 3 access points, and an AAA server.

Packet Tracer and EVE-NG are useful, and we use virtual tools too — the lab includes DNAC virtual access. But a simulator never gives you a boot loop at 7pm, a console cable that will not connect, or a fibre module seated wrong. Those are the problems working engineers get paid to solve, and you only learn them on hardware.

Routing & Switching Rack

3 routers, 3 Layer-3 switches, and 2 Layer-2 switches for hands-on topology building every lab day.

Security & Wireless

3 next-generation firewalls, 2 wireless LAN controllers, and 3 access points — the same equipment classes named in the NIP modules.

Services & Management

AAA server plus DNAC virtual access for authentication and controller-based management practice.

The Unglamorous Bits

Fibre and Ethernet modules, patch cables, and console cables — because cabling mistakes are half of real-world troubleshooting.
