Of all the questions a prospective CCNA candidate asks us at the KPHB counter, this is the one with the most misinformation attached to it. Students arrive having read three or four articles, convinced the Cisco exam will cost them around ₹25,000. Then they open the Pearson VUE checkout and the number is nearly ₹10,000 higher, and the first thing they feel is that somebody misled them.

Somebody did, though not deliberately. The pages ranking for this search were written months ago, converted US$300 at whatever the rupee was doing then, and left out a tax that Pearson VUE has been charging Indian candidates since 2017. One of them, dated 16 July 2026, still prints ₹24,500 to ₹26,000. Another, updated 14 May 2026, settles on ₹26,500.

So here is the arithmetic, with both sources named and the rate dated. We also checked what the exam costs relative to training in Hyderabad, read Cisco’s own retake policy rather than paraphrasing it, and looked at the version deadline sitting four months away that not one page-one result mentions.

How much does the CCNA exam cost in India in 2026?

The CCNA 200-301 exam costs US$300 plus tax, which for a candidate testing in India means US$354 after 18% India GST, or about ₹34,115 at 96.3704 rupees to the dollar on 7 October 2026. That is one attempt, paid to Cisco through Pearson VUE, and it covers no training, no books and no lab.

Why does ₹34,115 differ from the ₹25,000 figure on most pages?

Two compounding errors: an old dollar rate and an omitted 18% GST. A page that converted US$300 at 83 rupees and stopped there lands near ₹25,000 and feels right. Add the four rupees the dollar has gained and the tax Pearson VUE charges, and the same exam is ₹34,115 — a 29% understatement on the figure ranking first.

This is not a nitpick about rounding. It is the difference between a student who budgets correctly and one who reaches checkout short of money. Here is what page one of the India query was publishing when we read it on 7 October 2026, against the figure our arithmetic produces.

The strangest version of the error is Google’s own. When we expanded the People Also Ask question “How much does a CCNA test cost?” on 7 October 2026, the generated answer said, in a single sentence: “India: Approximately ₹25,000 to ₹30,000 INR (The standard $300 base fee plus an 18% mandatory GST brings the checkout total to $354 USD).” Both halves cannot be true. US$354 at that day’s rate is ₹34,115. The dollar figure it calculated itself is right; the rupee range it inherited from the pages it was citing is wrong.

Watch for the word “including”

Two page-one results describe their ₹25,000–₹30,000 range as already including tax. Cisco’s own pricing FAQ lists every level — entry US$125, associate US$300, professional US$400 core and US$300 concentration, expert US$450 written and US$1,600 lab — and attaches the words “plus tax” to all of them. Tax sits on top. A quoted rupee figure that is below US$300 at today’s rate cannot be tax-inclusive.

Does GST apply to the CCNA exam fee in India?

Yes. Pearson VUE charges 18% India GST on business-to-consumer sales to candidates sitting tests in India, a policy in force for every booking made on or after 1 July 2017. GST is added at checkout rather than built into the US$300 headline, and it is not refunded if you fail.

One nuance matters if your employer is paying. Pearson VUE’s own GST FAQ states that GST is not charged on sales made to businesses in India, with the reverse-charge mechanism applying to registered business customers instead. So a company booking against its GSTIN handles the tax differently from a student paying with a personal card. If your organisation is sponsoring the attempt, ask whoever raises the purchase order which route they are using before you budget for it yourself.

What does the CCNA exam fee actually buy you?

One 120-minute attempt at Implementing and Administering Cisco Solutions (200-301 CCNA) v1.1, delivered in English or Japanese through Pearson VUE, either at a test centre or online with a proctor. Nothing else. No study guide, no practice test, no lab access, and no second chance if the score falls short.

That is worth stating plainly because the phrase “CCNA cost” gets used for two completely different things. The Cisco exam fee is a fixed, non-negotiable charge paid to a certification body. Training fees are a separate, highly variable charge paid to an institute. One page-one result quotes “the average CCNA fee in India” as ₹7,000 to ₹15,000, which is its own course price, not the exam. Keeping the two apart is the single most useful thing you can do before you start comparing anything.

How much does a CCNA retake cost in India?

A retake costs the full ₹34,115 again, because Cisco sells attempts rather than passes. Its exam policy also imposes a wait: candidates who fail an associate-level exam must wait five calendar days, beginning the day after the failed attempt, before they may retest the same exam number.

Budget for this honestly. If you book the attempt before you are ready and fail, your CCNA has cost ₹68,230 before you have earned anything. That is more than most Hyderabad training programmes charge for several months of instruction, which is why we tell students at the demo to delay the booking until they are passing full-length practice exams rather than to book early as motivation.

Two more policy details worth knowing. If you pass and later want to retake the same associate-level exam number, Cisco requires a minimum 180-day wait. And the no-show and cancellation rules on lab exams are harsher still — full payment forfeited, with a 30-day block on rescheduling — so build the habit of treating a booked slot as money already spent.

Should you book CCNA v1.1 now or wait for v2.0 in February 2027?

Book now if you are within a few months of being ready. Cisco published the CCNA v2.0 exam topics on 20 May 2026, v2.0 goes live on 3 February 2027, and 200-301 v1.1 is testable only until 2 February 2027. A CCNA earned under v1.1 is a full CCNA, valid for three years, and nothing about it expires early.

We flagged this on the syllabus post and it is worth repeating here because it affects money, not just study plans. Not one page-one result for “ccna exam cost in india” mentions the version change at all. If you are six months from ready, you will be sitting v2.0 whether you intend to or not, and the five-domain v2.0 blueprint is a different weighting from the six-domain v1.1 one — so buying a v1.1-aligned course bundle in late 2026 is a decision worth making deliberately.

What are the hidden costs beyond the CCNA exam voucher?

Beyond the ₹34,115 attempt, a realistic CCNA budget adds study material, practice exams and lab access, which together run from nothing to roughly ₹25,000 depending on how much you buy. Cisco Packet Tracer is free. Official Cisco Press guides, commercial practice exams and paid lab sandboxes are not.

Official study guides

Cisco Press official cert guides typically run US$40 to US$90 each, so roughly ₹3,900 to ₹8,700. Optional, but the single most common purchase.

Practice exams

Commercial mock-exam engines run around US$75 to US$150, about ₹7,200 to ₹14,500. Worth it, because they are what tell you to stop delaying the booking.

Lab access

Cisco Packet Tracer is free. Paid sandboxes and virtual lab subscriptions are not. Physical equipment is why most learners pick a classroom programme instead.

Card and FX charges

Paying a dollar-denominated fee on an Indian card usually adds a foreign-transaction markup of a percent or two. Not large, but not zero either.

The retake you hope not to need

₹34,115 again, after a five-day wait. The honest way to budget a first attempt is to make the retake unnecessary rather than to save for it.

Recertification in three years

CCNA is valid for three years. Renewal means another exam or Cisco continuing-education credits, so the ₹34,115 is a three-year cost, not a once-ever one.

    Can you get the CCNA exam cheaper, or for free?

    No, the 200-301 attempt is never free, but there are three legitimate ways to reduce what you pay. Cisco accepts Cisco Learning Credits in place of cash, sells exam vouchers for associate-level exams through its own store, and runs periodic promotions. Everything outside those channels deserves suspicion.

    What is real, and what is not

    Cisco Learning Credits — Cisco’s own exam page states the CCNA costs US$300 or you can use Cisco Learning Credits. If your employer holds credits, use them.
    Cisco’s own voucher store — Cisco notes it offers exam vouchers for associate-level exams. Buy from Cisco’s store, not a reseller on a marketplace.
    Promotions, when they exist — Cisco has run second-attempt safeguard offers in the past. Verify any such offer on a cisco.com page with a current date before you count on it; the older safeguard landing page we checked had expired in October 2023.
    Free attempts, dumps and heavily discounted third-party vouchers — not a saving. Exam dumps breach Cisco’s exam policies and risk your certification. A voucher at a suspicious discount is the usual shape of a scam.

    The genuine saving is not on the voucher. It is on the number of attempts. One pass at ₹34,115 beats a discounted voucher plus a retake every time.

    How do you book the CCNA exam from Hyderabad?

    Create a Cisco certification profile, then schedule the 200-301 exam through Pearson VUE, choosing either a Hyderabad test centre or online proctored delivery. Payment is in dollars by card, with the 18% India GST added at checkout, and the slot is confirmed only once the payment clears.

    A few practical things we tell students at the KPHB campus before they book. Check your identification matches your Cisco profile name exactly, because a mismatch at the centre ends the attempt. If you choose online proctored delivery, test your room and webcam setup in advance rather than on the morning. And treat the slot as spent money from the moment you book it, because Cisco’s policies on cancellation and no-shows are not forgiving.

    Finally, pick the date against the February 2027 deadline. If you book a slot on or before 2 February 2027 you sit 200-301 v1.1. From 3 February 2027 you sit v2.0. Both earn you the same CCNA, but you want to sit the one you studied.
