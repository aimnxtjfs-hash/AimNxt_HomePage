On 3 October 2026 we read every result on Google’s first page for “ccna online training”, localised to India. There is no AI Overview. Cisco’s own Learning Network sits first. Second is a paid Indian provider at ₹17,999 plus GST. Third — above every institute in the country — is a free YouTube course.

That ordering tells you something before you read a word of the pages themselves. Google cannot find a strong reason to prefer the paid options, so it is hedging: here is the certification body, here is a seller, here is the same syllabus for nothing. If you are trying to decide what to actually buy, that page is not much help.

So we did the comparison the SERP will not do. We opened each option and wrote down only what it publishes: how many hours it teaches, how many of those hours are live, what its lab contains, what it costs, and which version of the exam it prepares you for. Three of those five answers are missing from most pages. The fifth answer is missing from all of them, and in October 2026 it is the one that matters most. Everything below is that comparison, followed by how the same syllabus is taught inside the AimNxt CCNA and CCNP networking course in Hyderabad, where Module 01 is CCNA and the lab is a rack you can put your hands on.

Is CCNA online training worth doing, or do you need a classroom?

CCNA online training is worth doing when it includes live teaching hours and configurable devices, and close to worthless when it is only recorded video. The CCNA 200-301 exam is a single 120-minute paper across six domains that tests configuration judgement, and judgement comes from console time rather than screen time.

The useful distinction is not online versus classroom. It is supervised versus unsupervised. In a live online session a trainer watches you type, sees the mistake, and makes you fix it — which is the entire mechanism by which networking is learned. A recording cannot do that, and no amount of production value changes it.

Which means the question to ask a provider is never “is this online?” It is “how many hours will a trainer be watching what I do, and on what equipment?” Everything else on a course page is decoration. The next five sections are about getting a straight answer to that one question.

Which CCNA exam version will your online course actually prepare you for?

Cisco published new CCNA exam topics on 20 May 2026, the new exam goes live on 3 February 2027, and the current 200-301 v1.1 exam can be taken only through 2 February 2027. No course page on Google’s first page for this keyword mentions any of those three dates.

This is the single most important fact for anyone buying CCNA training in October 2026, and you will not find it on the pages selling it to you. In Cisco’s own announcement, published 20 May 2026, the company describes the first major CCNA blueprint update since 2019, built around four pillars: network infrastructure, troubleshooting and problem-solving, a security-first mindset, and the role of AI in network management. Cisco released the new exam topics immediately and gave learners a deliberately long runway before the exam changes.

Read that calendar against your own plan and three situations fall out:

Sitting before 2 February 2027

Study v1.1 and book the exam now. Roughly four months of runway is comfortable for a working professional and tight for a fresher starting from zero. Any material you buy should state that it covers v1.1.

Sitting after 3 February 2027

You will take the new version, so a self-paced v1.1 video library bought today will be partly out of date at the moment you need it. Ask the provider in writing how and when it updates.

Not sure yet

Choose live teaching over recordings. A trainer teaches the current blueprint on the day you attend; a library teaches whatever it was recorded against, and nobody tells you when that was.

One question that filters the market

Ask any CCNA online course: “Does this cover 200-301 v1.1, and what happens to my access when v2.0 goes live on 3 February 2027?” A provider teaching this properly answers in a sentence. A provider selling recordings tends to change the subject. The only page-one result on this keyword that names a version at all is the free YouTube course.

Can you get CCNA training for free?

Yes, and the best free CCNA course outranks every paid Indian provider on Google. Jeremy’s IT Lab’s complete free CCNA v1.1 course sits third on the first page, above Network Kings, Udemy and every institute. Simplilearn gives five free hours with a completion certificate. Free gets you the syllabus, not a trainer.

We are not going to pretend otherwise, because you can see the ranking yourself. If your only constraint is money and you have high self-discipline and an existing IT job to practise in, the free route genuinely works, and a reputable paid provider should be able to say why it is worth more than free without flinching.

Here is the honest division of labour. Free material is excellent at explaining concepts and is usually more current than a paid recording, because a creator updates a playlist faster than an institute re-records a course. What free cannot give you is the four things below.

Someone who answers your specific question. Not a comment thread — a trainer who looks at your running config and tells you what is wrong with it this evening.
Equipment you could not otherwise touch. A wireless LAN controller, a next-generation firewall and an AAA server are not things a learner buys. Shared lab access is most of what you are paying for.
A sequence with a deadline. Free courses are finished by a small minority of the people who start them. A batch with dates and a trainer expecting you is a completion mechanism.
Interview preparation by people who sit on the other side of it. Mock interviews and resume work are a formal module in the AimNxt programme, not an afterthought.

If a paid course cannot clearly beat a free YouTube playlist on those four points, it is not worth its fee. That is a fair test and we would rather you apply it to us than not apply it at all.

Live online classes or self-paced video: which works better for CCNA?

Self-paced video is better for learning the theory and live classes are better for passing the exam, because the exam tests decisions you only learn to make when someone corrects you. Recorded content never notices that your subnet mask is wrong. A live trainer notices in the same minute you type it.

In practice the two are not rivals and the strongest learners we see use both. They watch a recording before the session so the live hour is spent on configuration rather than definitions, then they use the live hour to be wrong in front of someone. That sequence is worth more than twice as much of either one alone.

What do CCNA online courses cost in India, and what does the exam cost?

The CCNA 200-301 exam costs US$300 paid directly to Cisco, and that is separate from any course fee you pay a training provider. Published online course prices we read on 3 October 2026 ranged from ₹12,440 at UniNets to ₹17,999 plus GST at Network Kings.

The exam fee is the figure most often left out of a comparison, and at current rates it is larger than several of the course fees on this page. It goes to Cisco, not to your training provider, and no institute can waive it. Any budget that does not include it is incomplete.

Can you pass CCNA in one month of online training?

One month is enough only if you already work with networks daily; for a fresher it is the most common reason we see behind a failed first attempt. The blueprint spans six domains and the paper runs 120 minutes. Reading about OSPF for four weeks does not teach you to troubleshoot it.

Google shows this as a People Also Ask question on the keyword, which tells you how many people are hoping the answer is yes. For someone already administering a network, four focused weeks against v1.1 is realistic because most of the blueprint is describing their day job back to them. For a fresher the limit is not reading speed, it is the number of times you have broken something and fixed it.

A more honest planning frame, and the one we use with students:

1
Weeks 1–3: fundamentals and access. Addressing, subnetting until it is automatic, switching, VLANs. Configure every one of them yourself rather than watching them configured.
2
Weeks 4–7: IP connectivity and services. Static and dynamic routing, OSPF, NAT, DHCP, NTP. This is where lab hours stop being optional.
3
Weeks 8–10: security fundamentals and automation. Access control, device hardening, wireless security concepts, and the automation and programmability domain most candidates leave until it is too late.
4
Weeks 11–12: troubleshooting under time pressure. Broken topologies with a clock running. Cisco’s new blueprint names troubleshooting as a pillar, so this is also preparation for the exam you may actually sit.

That is roughly three months at a working professional’s pace, which is also why the AimNxt networking programme is built as months rather than weeks. What does the CCNA 200-301 syllabus cover?

Cisco’s CCNA 200-301 v1.1 exam covers six domains: network fundamentals, network access, IP connectivity, IP services, security fundamentals, and automation and programmability. Any online course worth paying for teaches all six and labs at least four of them. The exam itself runs 120 minutes in English or Japanese.

Those six domain names come from Cisco and the certification is valid for three years. They are also, word for word, the six sub-topics inside Module 01 of the AimNxt Networking Ignite Programme, because a training provider has no business renaming a vendor’s blueprint. When you compare syllabi across providers, you are really comparing how much lab time sits behind each identical heading.
