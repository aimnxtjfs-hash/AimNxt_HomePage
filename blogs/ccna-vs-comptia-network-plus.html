We get this question in the demo room most weeks, usually in the same words: “Someone told me to do Network+ first and then CCNA. Is that right?” It is a fair question, because that is what the internet says. Read the comparison articles on Google’s first page and they agree almost word for word. Network+ is vendor-neutral and beginner-friendly, CCNA is vendor-specific and advanced, so start with Network+ and work up.

On 4 October 2026 we went and checked that advice against the only two sources that can settle it: the two certification bodies’ own pages. The result was not what we expected. CompTIA states the recommended experience for Network+ as the A+ certification plus nine to twelve months of hands-on work in a junior network administrator or network support role. Cisco states the prerequisites for the 200-301 CCNA exam as none. The certificate everyone calls the beginner option formally asks for a prior certificate and the best part of a year on the job. The one everyone calls advanced asks for nothing.

The price goes the same way. Network+ retails at US$369; CCNA costs US$300. And when we looked at what Hyderabad employers actually type into a job advert, the gap got wider still. What follows is that comparison in full, including where each exam genuinely wins, and then how the same syllabus is taught inside the AimNxt CCNA and CCNP networking course in Hyderabad, where CCNA is Module 01 and the lab is a rack you can put your hands on.

Should a beginner do CCNA or CompTIA Network+ first?

For a beginner in India aiming at a networking job, CCNA is the better first certification, and both vendors’ own pages support that. Cisco lists no prerequisites for the 200-301 CCNA exam. CompTIA recommends A+ plus nine to twelve months of hands-on experience before Network+. Naukri showed 669 CCNA roles in Hyderabad on 4 October 2026.

That is the short answer, and it holds for most people reading this page. It is not universal. There are three situations where Network+ really is the right first move, and we go through them below rather than pretending the choice is obvious.

What is obvious is that the usual framing is wrong. “Network+ is easier so do it first” treats the two exams as rungs on one ladder. They are not. They are two different questions asked by two different kinds of employer, and the one you answer first should depend on which employer you are trying to reach.

Which certification actually expects less experience from you?

CompTIA Network+ formally expects more prior experience than CCNA, which is the opposite of what almost every comparison article says. CompTIA’s own Network+ page lists recommended experience as the A+ certification plus nine to twelve months in a junior network administrator role. Cisco’s CCNA exam page states prerequisites plainly: none.

Go and read both pages yourself, because this is the single fact that reframes the whole decision and it is sitting in plain sight on the vendors’ websites. On the CompTIA Network+ certification page, under exam details, “Recommended experience” reads: CompTIA A+ certification, with 9 to 12 months of hands-on experience in a junior network administrator or network support technician role. On Cisco’s own CCNA exam topics page, the exam details list prerequisites as none.

Neither of those is a hard gate. CompTIA will happily sell you a Network+ voucher with no experience at all, and plenty of people pass it that way. But when a vendor publishes recommended experience, it is telling you what level the questions are pitched at. Network+ is pitched at someone who has already been doing the job for most of a year. That is worth knowing before you pick it as your starting point.

Which costs more, CCNA or CompTIA Network+?

CompTIA Network+ costs more than CCNA: US$369 retail against US$300 for the 200-301 CCNA exam. CompTIA publishes the 369 figure in its own N10-009 explainer dated 18 December 2024, and Cisco prints US$300 on the CCNA exam-topics page. The cheaper exam is the one everyone calls advanced.

At roughly ₹88 to the dollar that is about ₹32,500 for Network+ against about ₹26,400 for CCNA, before any training. Convert it however you like; the ordering does not change. And it matters more than a sixty-nine dollar gap suggests, because the standard advice is not “pick one”. It is “do Network+ first, then CCNA”. Follow that and you are not comparing $369 with $300. You are comparing $669 with $300.

There is a renewal cost on both sides too. Both certifications are valid for three years. Cisco lets you recertify by passing a qualifying exam or by earning continuing-education credits. CompTIA renews through continuing-education credits, with CE tokens priced at $25 and $50 each on its own resources page. Neither is expensive on its own, but a beginner holding two certificates is maintaining two renewal clocks instead of one.

The cost nobody adds up

The sequence everyone recommends is A+, then Network+, then CCNA. Priced at retail from the vendors’ own pages that is three certificates, two of them bought mainly to qualify you for the third, and the third has no prerequisites at all. If your goal is a networking role and your budget is finite, that ordering costs you money and months without adding anything an interviewer will ask about.

What does each exam actually test?

CCNA 200-301 v1.1 tests six domains weighted towards configuration, while CompTIA Network+ N10-009 tests five domains weighted towards troubleshooting and concepts. CCNA puts 25 percent on IP Connectivity, meaning routing you configure. Network+ puts 24 percent on troubleshooting and 23 percent on networking concepts, with no vendor command line anywhere in it.

This is where the two certifications stop being rungs on a ladder and start being different things. Put the published blueprints side by side and the difference in intent is immediate.

Look at the top line of each column. CCNA’s heaviest domain is IP Connectivity, which is static and dynamic routing, OSPF, route selection and first-hop redundancy, configured and verified on a device. Network+’s heaviest domain is troubleshooting methodology: identify the problem, establish a theory, test it, implement, verify, document. Both are valuable. Only one of them involves typing a command.

Network+ also carries a genuine advantage that CCNA does not, and it is worth stating plainly. Its operations domain covers documentation, life-cycle management, change management, configuration baselines and disaster recovery with RPO, RTO, MTTR and MTBF. CCNA barely touches that process layer. If you are heading into an IT operations or service-desk career rather than a network-engineering one, that 19 percent is the most useful block of content in either syllabus.

Which is harder, CCNA or CompTIA Network+?

CCNA is harder, and the gap is in what the exam asks you to do rather than how much you must memorise. Both exams run around two hours; CCNA is 120 minutes and Network+ is 90. CCNA asks you to configure and verify Cisco devices. Network+ asks whether you understand how networks behave.

The most useful description of that gap we have heard did not come from a training provider. It came from a reply on the r/ccna thread that Google ranks in its discussions block for this exact query: Network+ is long division, CCNA is calculus. Another reply in the same thread puts it as Network+ being an overview, mostly theory, while CCNA is “let’s configure”. Those are the comments of people who have sat both exams, which is more than most comparison pages can claim.

In our batches the pattern is consistent. Candidates do not fail CCNA because the theory defeated them. They fail because they studied it the way you study Network+ — reading, watching, highlighting — and then met a question that asked what the device would do, which you only know if you have made a device do it. The difficulty is not conceptual. It is a difficulty of hours spent at a console.

Network+ rewards reading

Five domains, 90 minutes, a maximum of 90 questions, pass at 720 out of 900. Broad coverage of concepts, topologies, ports and protocols. You can prepare well without touching enterprise hardware.

CCNA rewards configuring

Six domains, 120 minutes, pass or fail, results in 48 hours. Performance-based questions and drag-and-drop alongside multiple choice. Lab hours are what separate a pass from a retake.

Neither is a shortcut

Network+ being easier to pass does not make it a faster route to CCNA. The CCNA blueprint assumes nothing, so the fundamentals you learn for Network+ get taught again in Module 01 anyway.

Why does every comparison tell you to do Network+ first?

Because almost every page ranking for this comparison is written for the American market, where CompTIA Network+ appears in job adverts and in United States Department of Defense role requirements. CompTIA’s own page lists DoD 8140 work roles for Network+. India has no equivalent mandate, so advice written for Virginia transfers badly to Hyderabad.

This is not a criticism of those pages. They are correct for their readers. In the United States, CompTIA certifications are embedded in hiring frameworks: the Network+ page itself names the NICE and DoD 8140 work roles it maps to, which means a federal contractor can be contractually required to employ certified staff. When a certificate is written into procurement rules, it gets written into job adverts, and recommending it first is sound advice.

None of that machinery exists in India. There is no Indian equivalent of DoD 8140 pointing at CompTIA, and Indian enterprise networks are overwhelmingly built on Cisco, with Palo Alto and Fortinet firewalls alongside. So the advice arrives here with its foundation removed, and gets repeated anyway because it sounds sensible and because it is what the top-ranking pages say.

One more thing worth noticing about this search result. On 4 October 2026 there was no AI Overview on “ccna vs comptia network+” at all. Google’s first web result was a Reddit thread, with a separate discussions and forums block carrying two more, and only one Indian site anywhere on page one. When Google ranks forum replies above professionally written comparisons, it is telling you that it cannot find a page it trusts more than people who have actually done it.

Does CompTIA Network+ get you hired in India?

CompTIA Network+ is poorly recognised by Indian job boards, while CCNA is a working search filter. On 4 October 2026 Naukri returned 669 CCNA vacancies in Hyderabad, 382 of them inside the Engineering Hardware and Networks department. The same site’s CompTIA searches collapse into noise, returning BPO, telecaller and sales roles.

We want to be careful here, because this is the kind of claim people make without evidence and the evidence is more interesting than the claim. Here is exactly what we did and exactly what came back, all on 4 October 2026, all for Hyderabad and Secunderabad.

This is also why we point beginners at the question of whether CCNA is still worth it in 2026 rather than at the vendor-neutral debate, because recognition and merit are separate things and only one of them gets you the call.

How many Hyderabad employers hire freshers on CCNA?

Hyderabad’s fresher window for CCNA is real but narrow: 52 vacancies open to zero years of experience out of 669 in total. Filter those 52 and only 26 sit in the networking department and 19 in the IT Network role category. At three years the same search returns 300 roles, nearly six times as many.

We publish this number every time because it is the one the brochures leave out, and a beginner deserves it before choosing a certificate rather than after. Fifty-two city-wide listings open to a true fresher is not nothing, but it is a queue, and about half of those 52 are not really networking jobs at all: 13 fall under Customer Success, Service and Operations and 6 under a Voice or Blended role category, which is technical support with CCNA listed as a nice-to-have.

Do you need CompTIA Network+ if you already have CCNA?

No. If you already hold CCNA you do not need CompTIA Network+, because CCNA covers the same fundamentals and goes further into configuration. The exception is a job advert that names Network+ specifically, which in India is rare. Adding it costs US$369 and buys you a credential your CCNA already implies.

This is one of the real People Also Ask questions Google shows on this search, and it is usually asked by someone part-way through a certification plan who is worried about a gap. There is no gap. Compare the blueprints above and Network+’s concepts, implementation and security domains are all territory CCNA covers, mostly in more depth and always with a device attached.

The direction that does make sense is the reverse. If you already hold Network+ and are now choosing what comes next, CCNA is the natural step, and your Network+ preparation will have genuinely shortened the first two CCNA domains. Nothing about that effort is wasted. The argument in this article is only about ordering when you have not started yet.

The one piece of Network+ content with no CCNA equivalent is its operations domain: change management, configuration baselines, documentation standards, and disaster-recovery metrics. If you are already in a networking job and your weakness is process rather than configuration, that domain is a reasonable thing to study. Whether it is worth $369 and an exam booking is a judgement only you can make.

Should you wait for CCNA v2.0 in February 2027?

Do not wait, but do pick a side of the date before you start studying. Cisco’s exam-topics page states the last day to test for 200-301 v1.1 is 2 February 2027 and the first day for v2.0 is 3 February 2027. Doing Network+ first typically burns the four months you have left.

This is the practical cost of the detour, and it is the part of the decision that is time-sensitive rather than permanent. If you start studying in October 2026 and sit CCNA directly, four months is a comfortable runway for a working professional and a tight one for a fresher starting from zero. If you insert Network+ first, you will realistically sit CCNA in 2027, on the new blueprint, having paid for two exams.

Neither version is better. Cisco published the new exam topics in May 2026 and the refresh brings the blueprint up to date on troubleshooting, a security-first approach and the role of AI in network management. The point is only that the version you study must match the version you sit, and a plan with an extra certificate wedged into it is far more likely to cross that line by accident. If you want the detail on what the refresh changes, we have covered the CCNA and CCNP certification comparison for 2026 separately.

One question to ask any training provider

“Does your syllabus cover 200-301 v1.1 or v2.0, and what happens to my access when the exam changes on 3 February 2027?” A provider teaching this properly answers in a sentence. A provider selling a recorded library tends to change the subject. Get the answer by email, not on a call.

Is CCNA still worth it in 2026?

CCNA remains the networking credential Indian employers filter on, which is the practical test of whether a certificate is worth doing. Cisco is refreshing it rather than retiring it, with new exam topics published in May 2026. It is worth doing when you pair it with lab fluency, because the certificate alone has never passed an interview.

That last clause matters more than the rest of the paragraph. We see both failure modes in the demo room. One is the candidate with no certificate who cannot get past the keyword screen. The other is the candidate with a certificate and no console hours, who gets the interview and then cannot answer what happens when a port goes into blocking state. The second is more painful because it looked like progress.

So the answer is yes with a condition attached. CCNA is worth it as a door-opener in a market that genuinely screens on it, which 669 Hyderabad listings on a single day supports. It is not worth it as a line on a CV that nothing backs up.
