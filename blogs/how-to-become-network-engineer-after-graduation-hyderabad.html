How to become a network engineer after graduation in Hyderabad

If you are new to networking, start with CCNA. If you already understand routing, switching, subnetting, VLANs and basic troubleshooting, CCNP is the better next step. CCNA builds your foundation. CCNP proves you can work with larger, more complex enterprise networks. Both sit inside AimNxt’s networking course in Hyderabad, taught from the KPHB 5th Phase campus in Kukatpally on physical Cisco lab equipment.

You do not need to be perfect on day one. But you do need practical confidence. Networking interviews quickly reveal whether you have only read theory or actually practiced. Understanding topics like CCNA vs CCNP can also help you choose the right certification path based on your career goals and technical expertise.

Role Overview

What does a network engineer do?

A network engineer helps build, support, monitor and troubleshoot computer networks. Depending on the role, you may work on:

Routers
Switches
IP addressing
VLANs
Routing protocols
Firewalls
Wireless access points
VPNs
Network monitoring tools
Troubleshooting tickets
User connectivity issues
In entry-level roles, you may not design large networks immediately. You may start with monitoring, support, configuration checks, ticket updates and basic troubleshooting. That is normal. The goal is to build experience and move up. Choosing the right networking course Hyderabad can help you build practical skills and prepare for real-world networking careers.
Learning Path

Your step-by-step learning journey

01

Learn networking fundamentals

Start with the basics. Do not skip this stage. You should understand:

what a network isOSI modelTCP/IP modelIP addressingsubnettingMAC address vs IP addressrouters vs switchesLAN, WAN and WLANDNS, DHCP and NATports and protocolsbasic security concepts
If subnetting feels hard in the beginning, that is common. Practise it daily until it becomes natural.
02

Learn CCNA-level routing and switching

CCNA is one of the best starting points for freshers because it gives structure to your networking foundation. Focus on:

network accessIP connectivityIP servicessecurity fundamentalsautomation basicsdevice configurationtroubleshooting
You do not need to chase only the certificate at first. First, learn the skill. Certification can support your resume, but interview confidence comes from practice.
03

Practise labs

This is where many students fall behind. Networking is not a subject you can learn only from PDFs. You need to practise commands, configurations and troubleshooting. Practise labs such as:

subnetting exercisesVLAN creationinter-VLAN routingstatic routingOSPF basicsACL configurationNAT configurationDHCP configurationswitch port troubleshootingrouter connectivity checks
Use packet simulators, virtual labs or physical lab access if available. If you join classroom training, ask how much hands-on time you will get.
04

Learn basic firewall and wireless concepts

Modern networking roles often touch security and wireless. Learn the basics of:

firewall rulesaccess controlNAT on firewallsVPN conceptswireless access pointswireless LAN controllersWPA2/WPA3 basicsnetwork segmentation
You do not need to become a firewall specialist immediately. But firewall and wireless exposure can make your resume stronger than a fresher who knows only basic routing and switching.
05

Build troubleshooting skill

Companies hire freshers who can think clearly during problems. Practise questions like:

A user cannot access the internet. What will you check first? A VLAN user cannot reach another VLAN. What could be wrong? A switch port is down. What are the possible reasons? DHCP is not assigning IP addresses. What will you check? A routing table does not show the expected route. What will you do?
Troubleshooting is not memorizing one answer. It is learning how to check step by step.
06

Prepare your resume

A fresher networking resume should not be empty or generic. Add:

networking skills learnedCCNA-level topicslab projectstools usedtroubleshooting scenarios practisedinternship or project experience if anysoft skills
Example resume project: "Configured a small office network with VLANs, inter-VLAN routing, DHCP, NAT and basic ACLs in a lab environment. Tested connectivity and documented troubleshooting steps."

That sounds stronger than writing only "knowledge of networking".

Interview Prep

Practise interview questions

Interviewers usually test basics first. Prepare questions on:

Focus on understanding the OSI model layers in depth and how the TCP/IP model differs. Be ready to explain the purpose of each layer with real-world examples.
OSI modelTCP vs UDPports and protocolsTCP/IP model
Subnetting is almost always tested. Be able to calculate subnet masks, determine host ranges, and identify network and broadcast addresses quickly.
subnettingIP addressingCIDR notationVLSM
Understand the difference between a router and switch, how VLANs segment networks, and how routing protocols decide the best path.
VLANsrouter vs switchOSPF basicsrouting protocols
These three services are core to any enterprise network. Know how each one works, common failure points, and how to troubleshoot them.
DHCPDNSNATACLs
Even at entry level, basic security knowledge is expected. Understand what a firewall does, what ACLs control, and how VPNs are used.
firewall basicsACLsVPNaccess control
Be prepared to walk through structured troubleshooting steps. Interviewers want to see your thinking process, not just the answer.
troubleshooting stepsswitch port issuesDHCP failuresrouting problems
Answer in simple language. Do not overcomplicate. Interviewers prefer clarity over memorized definitions.
