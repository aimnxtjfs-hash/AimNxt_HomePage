"Is CCNA still worth it?" comes up in almost every counselling session at our KPHB campus, usually from someone who has read one Reddit thread saying networking is dead and one advertisement saying CCNA guarantees a job. Both are wrong, and you deserve a straighter answer than either.

This post gives you that answer with numbers and sources: what CCNA actually gets you in India in 2026, where the certificate stops helping, what Cisco's February 2027 exam change means for your timing, and what to stack on top of it if you want the better salary bands. No hype in either direction.

Is CCNA Worth It in 2026? The Short Answer

Yes, CCNA is worth it in 2026 as a career foundation, but not as your only preparation. The Cisco Certified Network Associate (CCNA 200-301) still opens entry-level network jobs paying ₹3–5 LPA in India, provided you pair it with hands-on device skills, because employers now expect firewall, wireless, or automation exposure on top of the certificate.

That conditional matters. Hundreds of thousands of people in India hold a CCNA or equivalent knowledge, so the credential by itself no longer separates you from the crowd. What separates you is whether you can sit in front of a switch and make it do what the interviewer asks. The rest of this post unpacks when the investment pays off and when it disappoints.

One clarification before the details: Cisco is the certification body for the CCNA exam. Training institutes, including AimNxt, are independent providers that prepare you for it. Anyone who implies their institute issues the CCNA itself is being loose with the truth, which tells you something about their other claims too.

Is CCNA Still in Demand in India?

Networking demand in India remains steady in 2026, and CCNA is still the most recognised entry credential for it. Job portals list thousands of openings mentioning CCNA, and Gartner reports that 87% of IT leaders struggle to find qualified networking talent. Enterprise networks, global capability centres, and new data centre projects all need engineers who can configure real infrastructure.

The demand looks different from a decade ago, though. Pure "configure a router" roles are fewer; roles that mix routing and switching with firewalls, wireless, cloud connectivity, or automation are growing. Hyderabad reflects this clearly: the GCCs in HITEC City and Gachibowli and the data centre builds across Telangana hire network engineers constantly, but their interview checklists go beyond the CCNA blueprint.

So the accurate statement is not "CCNA gets you a job" and not "CCNA is dead." It is: CCNA-level fundamentals are the entry ticket, and the seats go to candidates who bring something extra to the table.

What Is the Real ROI of CCNA in India?

The CCNA 200-301 exam costs USD 300 plus taxes, roughly ₹27,000, and a fresher role after CCNA-level training typically pays ₹3–5 LPA in India, per PyNet Labs and AmbitionBox estimates from January 2026. Most students recover their full investment, exam and training included, within the first months of salary. These are market estimates, not guarantees.

The more useful ROI question is about time. A structured program takes three to four months. Compare that with a two-year master's degree or a six-month coding bootcamp, and networking remains one of the fastest legitimate routes from graduation to an IT payroll, especially for students who prefer working with systems over writing software all day.

For detailed pay bands by experience level and city, see our full breakdown of the CCNA salary in India for 2026. The short version: freshers start at ₹3–5 LPA, and engineers who add CCNP-level and multi-vendor skills reach ₹7–15 LPA within three to six years.

Count the lab hours, not just the exam fee

The exam fee is the smallest part of the investment. The return comes from hands-on hours: a candidate with 100+ hours on real routers, switches, and firewalls interviews in a different league from one who memorised dumps. Ask any program you evaluate exactly how many lab hours are included.

When Is CCNA Alone Not Enough?

CCNA alone is not enough when the role requires troubleshooting under pressure, multi-vendor environments, or security work. Interviewers rarely ask "what is OSPF"; they ask you to debug why an OSPF neighbour is down. Candidates who trained only for the multiple-choice exam struggle in practical rounds, which is where most fresher rejections happen.

Here is the honest gap, in the format interviews actually take:

Factor What CCNA Teaches What Interviews Test
VLANs Configure a VLAN on a switch Find out why VLAN 10 users lost the server yesterday
Routing Explain OSPF areas and states Debug an OSPF neighbour stuck in EXSTART, live
Security List security fundamentals Build a firewall policy for a real office network
Design Subnet a network on paper Plan addressing for a 200-user office with growth
Vendors Cisco IOS commands Comfort across Cisco, Palo Alto, Fortinet, Aruba

None of this makes the certification useless. It means the exam is the floor, not the ceiling. Training that stops at exam preparation leaves you exactly one practical round away from a rejection email, and that round is now standard at most Indian employers.

Should You Wait for the New CCNA v2.0 Exam in 2027?

No, do not wait. Cisco announced CCNA v2.0 in May 2026, but the new exam only goes live on February 3, 2027, and the current 200-301 v1.1 remains valid until then. A certification earned now stays valid for three years, and the routing, switching, and IP fundamentals you learn do not expire with the blueprint.

What is changing, per Cisco's official announcement: the v2.0 blueprint adds a section on AI in network operations, folds automation topics into it, and increases the weight of networking fundamentals and switching to half the exam. Troubleshooting gets more emphasis across the board.

Notice what that update rewards: exactly the hands-on fundamentals and troubleshooting practice this post keeps returning to. If you start a structured program now, you finish and certify on the current exam months before the switch, and everything you learned still maps onto v2.0. Waiting eight months to study a nearly identical syllabus just delays your first salary.

Who Should (and Shouldn't) Do CCNA?

CCNA suits B.Tech, BCA, and BSc graduates who want infrastructure careers, working IT support professionals moving into networking, and career changers who prefer systems over coding. It is a poor fit if you want a pure software development role, or if you expect the certificate alone to produce a job offer without lab practice.

Good fit: Fresh graduates

B.Tech, BCA, BSc (2024–26 batches) who want a defined, fast route into IT infrastructure roles.

Good fit: IT support pros

Desktop and helpdesk engineers upgrading into network roles with weekend or evening batches.

Good fit: Career changers

Non-IT backgrounds who like hands-on systems work. Networking hires on demonstrable skill, not just pedigree.

Poor fit: Pure developers

If your goal is software engineering, learn programming first. CCNA will not move that career.

Poor fit: Certificate collectors

Stacking paper credentials without lab hours fails at the practical round. Every time.

Poor fit: Overnight expectations

If you cannot commit three to four months of real practice, wait until you can. Half-done preparation wastes the fee.
