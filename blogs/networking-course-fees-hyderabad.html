A parent sat at the KPHB counter two weeks ago with three printed quotations. One institute wanted ₹6,500. One wanted ₹25,000. One wanted ₹50,000. All three pages said “hands-on”, all three said “placement support”, and all three said “industry-expert trainers”. He asked the only sensible question: what is the difference?

Nobody could tell him, because none of the three quotations said how many hours of teaching he was buying. That is the whole problem with networking course fees in Hyderabad. The prices are not wrong. They are just unmeasurable, and a price you cannot measure is a price you cannot compare.

So we did the arithmetic instead of the argument. On 9 October 2026 we read every published Hyderabad networking fee we could find, wrote down exactly what each page states, and then priced the two Cisco exams that this career path needs. What follows is that data, including the parts that are awkward for a training institute to publish.

How much are networking course fees in Hyderabad in 2026?

Published networking course fees in Hyderabad run from ₹3,000 to ₹60,000, a twenty-fold spread for courses described in almost identical words. On 9 October 2026 we read thirteen published fee sources for the city. Only one quoted a price per hour taught, which is why the range looks arbitrary.

That is the honest headline. The lower end buys a few weeks of recorded or group instruction on simulator software. The upper end usually buys live classroom teaching, and occasionally buys access to physical equipment. Which one you got is almost never stated on the page you paid from.

Why does the same networking course cost ₹3,000 at one institute and ₹60,000 at another?

The spread exists because a networking course fee in Hyderabad is quoted against no unit of measurement. Collegedunia’s own institute table lists Vision Networks at ₹3,000 and Edubridge at ₹60,000 with durations from twenty days to four months, and states no teaching hours for any of the seven institutes.

Compare that with how you buy anything else. Petrol has a litre. Rice has a kilo. Training has an hour, and almost nobody prints it. Collegedunia’s page even contradicts itself: the introduction says fees average “between INR 3,000 and INR 25,000”, while its own table immediately below lists Edubridge at ₹60,000.

There is a second reason, and it is more interesting. The word “networking course” covers at least four different products in Hyderabad: a short hardware-and-networking crash course, CCNA 200-301 preparation, CCNP-level enterprise work, and a multi-technology programme that adds firewalls and wireless. Those are not the same thing, so their prices should not match. The trouble is the pages do not say which one they are selling.

A fee range is not a market rate

When the published range for one city is 20x wide, that is not competition finding a price. It is missing information. Two institutes can quote ₹25,000 and mean forty hours and two hundred hours respectively, and nothing on either page lets you tell.

Of thirteen Hyderabad fee sources read on 9 October 2026, every one published a price and only three published anything you can divide it by. UrbanPro quotes ₹240 to ₹400 per hour, ACTE pairs ₹16,500 with forty-plus hours, and The Knowledge Academy pairs ₹44,995 with five days.

How much does a CCNA course cost in Hyderabad?

CCNA course fees quoted for Hyderabad on 9 October 2026 ranged from ₹5,000 for self-paced online study to ₹44,995 for a five-day instructor-led class. NG Cloud Networks puts the city’s typical band at ₹8,000 to ₹20,000. The aggregator Laimoon lists a far wider ₹9,615 to ₹3,65,356.

How much does the Cisco exam itself cost, and is it in the fee?

The Cisco exams are almost never included, and in India they cost more than the course. Cisco prices the CCNA 200-301 at US$300 and the CCNP ENCOR 350-401 at US$400, both plus tax. With 18% GST at ₹96.72 to the dollar on 9 October 2026, that is ₹79,891.

Cisco’s own exam pricing page lists associate-level exams at US$300 and professional core exams in the 350-series at US$400, in both cases followed by the words “plus tax”. Pearson VUE, which delivers the exams, charges 18% GST to candidates testing in India. Here is the arithmetic in full.

Only NG Cloud Networks says plainly that the training fee and the Cisco exam fee are separate, and even it does not give the exam price. Nobody else on either search result page mentions it. If you budgeted ₹20,000 for “a networking course” and nobody told you about this, your plan is short by roughly ₹34,000 before you even sit the first exam. Rates and prices move, so check both

The ₹79,891 figure is built from two things that change: Cisco’s USD list price and the rupee. We read the rate at ₹96.72 on 9 October 2026 from Google’s Morningstar-sourced converter. Recompute before you budget, and treat this as the method rather than a permanent number.

What is the real total cost of becoming a network engineer in Hyderabad?

Add the exams and the real total lands between roughly ₹85,000 and ₹1,40,000, not the ₹8,000 to ₹20,000 most Hyderabad fee pages quote. Ten of the thirteen published fees we read sit below ₹34,239, which is what one CCNA attempt alone costs a candidate testing in India.

There are four line items, and most people only budget for one of them:

The four line items in a real networking budget

Training fee — ₹3,000 to ₹60,000 in Hyderabad, depending entirely on hours and equipment you have to ask about.
Exam vouchers — ₹34,239 for CCNA 200-301, ₹45,652 for CCNP ENCOR 350-401, at 9 October 2026 rates. Usually not included.
Retakes — Cisco charges full price per attempt. One failed CCNA doubles that line to ₹68,478.
Your time — the item nobody prices. A 40-hour course and a 180-hour course are different parts of your year, not just different invoices.

One honest qualification: you do not have to sit both exams, and plenty of working network engineers never take ENCOR. The skills are what get hired; the certificate is what gets your CV past a filter. But if a course is sold to you as a CCNA-plus-CCNP path, the ₹79,891 belongs in the conversation on day one, not in month four.

How much do CCNP course fees in Hyderabad add on top?

Hyderabad providers rarely publish a separate CCNP fee at all, which is why PyNet Labs describes advanced networking courses in Ameerpet only as costing lakhs. The demand is real: Naukri listed 510 CCNP vacancies in Hyderabad and Secunderabad on 9 October 2026 against 670 naming CCNA.

That ratio is the part worth sitting with. CCNP is named in 76% as many Hyderabad adverts as CCNA. The professional-level certification is not a niche add-on in this city — it is asked for nearly as often as the associate one, and it is where the salary bands open up. Yet it is the level almost no Hyderabad fee page will quote a number for.

Practically, you will meet one of three pricing shapes. Some institutes sell CCNA and CCNP as two separate purchases, which means two fees and two negotiations. Some sell a combined track at a single fee, which is cheaper per hour but harder to compare. A few sell CCNP only to people who already hold CCNA. Ask which shape you are being sold before you compare any number to any other number.

Which networking course is best, and why does that change the fee?

No single networking course is best, but Hyderabad adverts price routing, firewalls and wireless together, so a one-technology fee and a four-technology fee are not comparable numbers. On 9 October 2026 Naukri showed 1,639 network engineer roles in the city, 906 naming firewalls and 1,972 naming wireless.

A caveat we owe you on those last two figures: job-board keyword matching is loose. “Wireless” in Hyderabad also catches telecom and RF roles that have nothing to do with enterprise Wi-Fi, and “Palo Alto” — 1,239 listings the same day — catches a city name as well as a firewall vendor. Treat them as evidence that the skills are advertised widely, not as precise counts.

What is not loose is the direction. Hyderabad enterprise networking adverts routinely bundle routing and switching with a firewall platform and a wireless platform, and they expect one person to hold all three. So when you put two quotations side by side, the first question is not “which is cheaper” but “how many of those three does each one actually teach”. A ₹16,500 CCNA course and a multi-technology programme are not the same purchase at different prices. They are different purchases.

What is the CCNA salary in India, and does the fee pay back?

AmbitionBox puts the typical Network Engineer salary in India at ₹4.9 lakh to ₹5.5 lakh a year, from 62,800 reported salaries, updated 8 October 2026. At one to three years of experience the figure is ₹3.7 lakh to ₹4 lakh. These are market estimates, not guarantees.

Those are self-reported, aggregated and nationwide, and they depend on experience, employer, city and how you perform in the interview. Nobody can promise you a number. What the figures do let you do is sanity-check a fee: a total outlay of ₹85,000 to ₹1,40,000 including exams sits against a first-role band in the ₹3.7 lakh to ₹4 lakh region, so on paper it is a few months of salary. That is a reasonable trade if the training is real and a bad one if it is not.

Be careful with any institute that quotes a salary as if it were an outcome of paying them. It is an outcome of the market, your skills and that interview.

What should you verify before you pay any networking institute in Hyderabad?

Ask for six numbers in writing before you pay: total teaching hours, lab hours inside that total, the physical device list, whether exam vouchers are included, batch size, and what the placement support actually commits to. Any institute that will not put those in writing has told you something.

The six numbers, and why each one matters

Total teaching hours. Without it you cannot compute cost per hour, which means you cannot compare two quotations at all. Ten of the thirteen sources we read do not publish it.
Lab hours inside that total. “180 hours” and “180 hours including labs” are different offers. Ask which it is.
The device list, by count. Routers, switches, firewalls, wireless LAN controllers, access points. If the answer is only software names, you are buying simulator time, which is free elsewhere.
Exam vouchers: included or not. This is a ₹34,239 question for CCNA and a ₹45,652 question for CCNP ENCOR.
Batch size. Three routers shared between eight students and between thirty students are not the same lab, whatever the equipment list says.
What placement support commits to. Interview opportunities, resume help and mock interviews are real and checkable. “100% placement” is not a commitment anybody can keep, and a written guarantee of a job should make you more suspicious, not less.

One last practical note on location, since it affects the real cost. If you are commuting from Miyapur, Bachupally, Nizampet or Kukatpally, a KPHB 5th Phase classroom is a short trip; from the other side of the city it is an hour each way, twice a day, for three to four months. That is unpaid time nobody puts in the fee, and it is a genuine reason to choose online or hybrid delivery over a slightly cheaper classroom across town.

Is a networking course fee in Hyderabad worth paying in 2026?

It is worth paying when the fee buys hours on equipment you will meet at work, and poor value when it buys weeks of simulator time you could have had free. Only 90 of 1,639 Hyderabad network engineer vacancies on 9 October 2026 were open to zero-experience candidates.

That 5.5% figure is the real argument for paying for training rather than self-studying, and also the real argument for being fussy about what you pay for. The fresher funnel in this city is narrow. What gets you through it is not the certificate on its own — it is being the candidate who has actually consoled into a switch, written a firewall policy on two different vendors’ kit, and joined an access point to a controller. That costs equipment and hours, and equipment and hours cost money.
