Search for SOC analyst salary in India and you get a wall of tidy tables. Fresher, mid-level, senior. City by city. Certification by certification. Almost none of them tell you where the numbers came from, and several of them quietly mix in a different job title to make the figures look better.

So this guide does the opposite. Every figure below is read off a named source with a date attached, and where the data says something unflattering — and it does — it stays in. We also answer the two questions Google shows next to this search that no salary page bothers with: whether the job runs 24x7, and whether it is stressful. That is part of what you are being paid for.

How Much Does a SOC Analyst Earn in India in 2026?

A SOC Analyst in India earns ₹5.2 lakh to ₹5.7 lakh per year at one to five years of experience, according to AmbitionBox data from 12,000 salaries updated on 7 September 2026. Glassdoor India puts the average at ₹5,15,500, with 90th-percentile earners near ₹11,28,000. These are market estimates, not guarantees.

Two independent aggregators landing within about ₹40,000 of each other is unusually good agreement, and it tells you the honest centre of gravity for this role sits a little above ₹5 lakh a year. AmbitionBox marks its own band "high confidence", which is what a 12,000-salary sample buys you.

One warning before you read further. On the same results page, a site called 6figr shows ₹17.8 lakh as an average — but for Security Analyst, a broader title that sweeps in GRC, application security and senior engineering roles. It is not the same job as an L1 SOC Analyst, and anchoring on it is the single most common reason freshers walk into interviews with the wrong expectation.

What Is the Starting Salary of a SOC Analyst in India?

A fresher SOC Analyst in India typically starts between ₹3.5 lakh and ₹6 lakh per year, and the honest middle of that band is around ₹4 lakh for a first L1 role at a large IT services employer. Indeed India reports Hyderabad SOC Analyst pay starting near ₹3,52,618 per year.

The spread inside that band is mostly about who hires you, not how good you are on day one. A campus-style intake at a big services firm anchors low. A product company or a specialist MSSP that interviews you on real investigation skill will start meaningfully higher — a live CrowdStrike L1 SOC Analyst posting in Hyderabad this month lists ₹8 lakh to ₹13 lakh, but asks for around two years and NGSIEM experience.

Freshers also frequently miss that the advertised figure is usually base pay. Rotating shifts normally carry a shift allowance on top, which can add a real amount over a year of night rotations. Ask about it explicitly — it is a standard question and interviewers expect it.

How Does SOC Analyst Salary Grow With Experience?

SOC Analyst pay in India rises slowly for the first two years and then jumps once you can investigate without a playbook. AmbitionBox puts one to five years at ₹5.2 lakh to ₹5.7 lakh, while a live CrowdStrike L1 opening in Hyderabad at two years advertises ₹8 lakh to ₹13 lakh.

That gap between the aggregate band and the live posting is the whole story of the first five years. The aggregate is flat because most of the 12,000 people in it are doing playbook triage at large employers. The postings that pay double are asking for something specific: you can pivot across log sources, you have touched a named SIEM in anger, and you can write up an investigation that another analyst can follow.

Does the City You Work In Change a SOC Analyst's Salary?

Barely. AmbitionBox's 12,000 salaries put Hyderabad at ₹5.2 lakh to ₹5.7 lakh per year, exactly level with Bengaluru, and the gap between the highest and lowest Indian metro is about ₹20,000 a year. City is the weakest lever on your SOC Analyst salary, not the strongest.

Practically, a Hyderabad fresher is not trading salary for staying home. Which Industries and Employers Pay SOC Analysts the Most?

Management consulting pays SOC Analysts the most in India at ₹6.8 lakh to ₹7.5 lakh per year, while emerging-technology firms pay the least at ₹4.4 lakh to ₹4.9 lakh. Inside IT services, AmbitionBox puts Cognizant at ₹6.2 to ₹6.9 lakh and Infosys at ₹4.7 to ₹5.2 lakh.

Line those two facts up against the city table and the picture inverts. The industry you sit in is worth about ₹2.5 lakh a year at the extremes. The employer you pick inside plain IT services is worth about ₹1.5 lakh. The city you live in is worth about ₹20,000. Most guides rank these in exactly the wrong order.

Is SOC Analyst Salary Actually Rising in India?

Not much at entry level. AmbitionBox records 2% growth in the average SOC Analyst salary in India across the two years from 2024 to 2026, which is below inflation. Demand for security staff is rising, but the L1 tier is crowded, and crowded tiers do not push pay upward quickly.

You will not find that 2% anywhere else on this search, and it is the most useful number on the page. It says the entry tier is not where the money is moving, so treating L1 as a destination is the mistake. Treat it as the two years in which you buy the skills that are moving.

It also explains the CrowdStrike-style postings. Employers are not paying more for triage — they are paying more for the small number of analysts who have gone past it. The market is stratifying, not lifting.

What Actually Raises a SOC Analyst's Salary?

Three things move a SOC Analyst's pay in India: leaving pure L1 triage for investigation work, picking one specialism such as detection engineering or cloud security, and moving into a higher-paying industry. The industry spread is roughly ₹2.5 lakh a year, about twelve times the spread between Indian cities.

Get past playbook triage

The pay jump is not at two years, it is at the point where you can investigate an alert the playbook does not cover and write it up cleanly.

Pick one specialism, early

Detection engineering, malware analysis, DFIR or cloud security. Depth in one is what the ₹8–13 LPA postings are screening for.

Change industry before you change city

Consulting, software product and telecom all pay above IT services on the same data. Relocating pays about ₹20,000.

Make your work visible

Documented investigations, a tuned detection rule, a home lab you can walk an interviewer through. Evidence beats claims in a salary conversation.

Certifications belong here too, with one caveat. CompTIA Security+ or Microsoft SC-200 mostly works as a recruiter filter — it gets your CV past the first screen, and the lab work behind it is what survives the technical round. CompTIA, EC-Council, Cisco and Microsoft are the certification bodies; AimNxt is an independent training provider that prepares you for their exams and issues its own certificate of completion.

Is SOC Analyst a 24/7 Job, and Is It Stressful?

Yes to both, and this belongs in any honest salary answer. A Security Operations Centre runs 24x7, so L1 analysts work rotating shifts including nights and weekends, and alert fatigue is the most common reason people leave the tier within two years. Shift allowances add to the base figures above.

Google surfaces both of these as related questions next to this exact salary search, which tells you something: people asking what the job pays are really asking whether the trade is worth it. No salary page currently ranking answers either one.

The honest version is that the stress is rarely technical. It comes from volume — a queue that refills faster than you clear it — and from a body clock that never settles. Teams with tuned detections, real playbooks and a fair rotation are markedly easier to work in, and you can ask about all three in an interview. Good SOC managers respect the question.

Questions worth asking before you accept an L1 offer

What is the shift rotation and the allowance on top of base? How many alerts does an L1 handle per shift? Is there a defined path from L1 to L2, and who moved last year? Which SIEM will I actually touch, and from which week?
