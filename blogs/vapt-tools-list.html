Search “VAPT tools” and you get lists. Top 7. Top 10. Top 20. They are mostly accurate and almost entirely useless to someone learning, because every one of them is written to sell a security audit to a company, not to tell a student what to install tonight.

We read the whole of Google’s first page for this query from India on 26 September 2026. Four sponsored blocks sit above the organic results. The depth leader runs roughly 7,500 words and lists twenty tools, two of which are the author’s own products. Not one page tells you which of these tools Indian employers actually name in job descriptions, what the free versions can and cannot do, or where each tool sits in an engagement.

So we went and checked. This guide ranks the core VAPT tools by how often they appear in Hyderabad job listings, states the free-tier limits in writing, maps every tool to the stage of an engagement it belongs to, and cites the tool list the Government of India’s own CERT-In empanelled auditor publishes. The numbers are ours, read live, and dated.

What Are the Essential VAPT Tools?

Six tools cover almost every Vulnerability Assessment and Penetration Testing engagement: Nmap for discovery, Nessus or OpenVAS for vulnerability scanning, Burp Suite for web application testing, Metasploit for exploitation, Wireshark for traffic analysis, and Kali Linux as the operating system that ships all of them. Everything beyond those six is specialisation you add later.

That list will not surprise anyone. What should surprise you is the order employers care about, which is not the order the tutorials teach. More on that in a moment.

A tool is not a skill

Nobody in a Hyderabad interview asks you to list tools. They ask what you found with one. “I ran Nessus” is not an answer; “Nessus flagged 412 findings on a /24, I stripped the informational noise, and three of the remaining eleven were externally reachable” is. Learn the tool to produce that sentence.

Where Does Each VAPT Tool Fit in the Engagement?

A penetration test runs in five stages — reconnaissance, vulnerability assessment, exploitation, post-exploitation, and reporting — and each stage has two or three tools that own it. Learning tools in that order beats learning them alphabetically, because the output of one stage is the input to the next.

This is the single most useful mental model you can carry into a first job. When a panel hands you a scenario, they are checking whether you know which stage you are in.

StageWhat you are doingThe tools that own it
1. ReconnaissanceFinding what exists before touching it — domains, subdomains, hosts, people, exposed services.theHarvester, Amass, Sublist3r, Maltego, Netdiscover
2. Scanning & enumerationMapping live hosts, open ports, services, versions; then scanning those services for known flaws.Nmap, Nessus, OpenVAS, Nikto, WPScan, Gobuster
3. ExploitationProving a flaw is real, by hand or with a framework. Web testing lives here too.Burp Suite, Metasploit, SQLMap, OWASP ZAP
4. Post-exploitationPrivilege escalation, credential harvesting, lateral movement, domain persistence.Mimikatz, Hashcat, John the Ripper, PowerView
5. ReportingTurning evidence into something a client can act on. No tool does this for you.Your notes, screenshots, CVSS scoring, a template

Stage ordering follows standard penetration testing methodology and matches the sequence the AimNxt VAPT & Ethical Hacking curriculum teaches. Wireshark sits across stages 1–4 rather than in any one of them, which is why it rarely appears in stage diagrams.

If the stages themselves are new to you, start with our explainer on what VAPT actually means and the breakdown of the types of penetration testing, then come back to the tools.

Which Tools Handle Reconnaissance and Discovery?

Reconnaissance splits into passive and active: theHarvester, Amass, Sublist3r and Maltego gather what is already public, while Nmap, Netdiscover and Gobuster touch the target directly. The AimNxt curriculum teaches the passive set first, because passive recon generates no logs and is the only stage you can safely practise against real domains.

Nmap is where most people start, and most people stop too early. Learning nmap -sV -sC against a lab box is an afternoon. Learning why a -sS scan behaves differently through a stateful firewall, when to accept the top-1000 default and when you need -p-, and how to read a service banner that is lying to you — that is the part interviews probe.

Subdomain enumeration deserves its own evening. Amass and Sublist3r find hosts an organisation forgot it owned, and forgotten hosts are where findings live. Gobuster does the same job one directory level down. In bug bounty work this stage decides the outcome before any scanner runs.

Nessus or OpenVAS — Which Vulnerability Scanner Should You Learn?

Learn Nessus for employability and OpenVAS for unlimited practice. Nessus appeared in 300 Hyderabad IT and Information Security listings on 26 September 2026; OpenVAS appears in job descriptions far less often but has no host limit, no licence clock, and runs free forever on a home lab.

The honest answer is that you need both, and for different reasons. Tenable’s Nessus is the commercial standard Indian enterprises and CERT-In empanelled auditors actually run, so the interface is worth knowing. OpenVAS is what you will use at 11pm against your own lab, because the free Nessus tier runs out fast.

Where beginners waste months is treating scanner output as the deliverable. A scanner produces a list of candidate findings. The job is validating them. Roughly a third of what a scanner reports on a mature estate is either a false positive or a finding the compensating control already neutralises, and the person who can tell the difference is the person who gets hired.

What Can You Actually Do With the Free Versions?

Nessus Essentials is free but capped at five IP addresses, issued as a 30-day licence, and its plugin feed runs 30 days behind the paid product. Verified on tenable.com on 26 September 2026. Burp Suite Community gives you the manual proxy but no automated scanner. These limits decide what you can practise, and no competing guide states them.

Burp Suite or OWASP ZAP for Web Application Testing?

Burp Suite is what Hyderabad employers name — 24 IT and Information Security listings on 26 September 2026 against OWASP ZAP’s 4 — but ZAP is fully free and Burp Suite Community withholds the automated scanner. Learn the Burp Suite workflow, and use ZAP where you need automation you cannot pay for.

They are not really competitors. ZAP has become the scanner teams drop into a build pipeline, which is why its four security listings sit alongside eleven software and QA ones. Burp Suite is what a human sits in front of for six hours. The interception proxy, Repeater and the request-editing loop are the actual skill, and that loop is identical in both tools.

If you are choosing where to put your hours, put them here. Web and API testing is where Indian application-security hiring is concentrated, and the VAPT & Ethical Hacking programme at AimNxt spends four of its fifteen modules on web security for exactly that reason — OWASP Top 10 fundamentals, injection attacks, authentication and authorisation flaws, and client-side and business logic bugs, each with lab work in Burp Suite against DVWA and bWAPP.

What Is Metasploit For, and Is It Still Worth Learning?

Metasploit is an exploitation framework: a catalogue of ready-written exploits, payloads and post-exploitation modules that turns a confirmed vulnerability into a shell. It appeared in only 8 Hyderabad security listings on 26 September 2026, but it remains worth learning because it is how you demonstrate impact rather than merely assert it.

The demand number is low for an unglamorous reason. Most Indian VAPT work is assessment work for compliance and client assurance, and a great deal of it stops at validated findings without weaponising them. Exploitation matters most in red team engagements, and those roles are a small slice of the market.

Learn it anyway, but learn it second. A candidate who can run a Metasploit module but cannot explain why the target was vulnerable has the sequence backwards, and the follow-up question in a technical round will expose that in about thirty seconds. Our guide to writing a penetration testing report covers how exploitation evidence turns into something a client can act on.

Do You Need Kali Linux to Run VAPT Tools?

No. Every major VAPT tool installs on Ubuntu, Windows or macOS, and Kali Linux is a convenience distribution that ships several hundred of them pre-configured. Kali named 386 Hyderabad IT and Information Security listings on 26 September 2026, which reflects it being industry shorthand for offensive security work rather than a hard requirement.

Run it in a virtual machine, not on your main laptop. A three-machine home lab — Kali as the attacker, a deliberately vulnerable box such as Metasploitable or DVWA, and a Windows box you join to a small domain — gets you further than any tool tutorial, because it lets you practise the sequence rather than the command.

One caution worth stating plainly: run these tools only against systems you own or have written permission to test. Scanning someone else’s infrastructure without authorisation is an offence under the Information Technology Act, 2000, and “I was learning” is not a defence. Authorisation is the first question in most VAPT interviews for a reason.

Which VAPT Tools Does the Government of India Use?

Software Technology Parks of India, a CERT-In empanelled auditor under the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology, publishes eight tools it uses for VAPT engagements: Wireshark, Nmap, OpenVAS, Aircrack, Metasploit, Nessus, Nipper Studio and a commercial Retina scanner. It states it runs around 50 certified VAPT resources pan-India.

That list is worth more than any vendor’s top-20 ranking, because it is an operational disclosure rather than marketing. Six of the eight are free or open source. Nipper Studio, the outlier, audits network device configurations — firewall, router and switch rulesets — which is a category most beginner guides omit entirely and which matters a great deal in Indian compliance work.

If you are targeting work with a CERT-In empanelled auditor in Hyderabad, read that list as a syllabus. Source: STPI’s official VAPT page, read on 26 September 2026.

What Do Beginners Get Wrong About VAPT Tools?

The three costly mistakes are collecting tools instead of finishing engagements, treating scanner output as a deliverable, and practising exploitation before understanding the protocol underneath it. All three produce a candidate who can demonstrate a tool in a lab and cannot answer the follow-up question in a Hyderabad technical round.

Tool collecting is the most common. A learner installs forty tools from a YouTube list, runs each once, and has nothing to describe in an interview. Six tools used properly across three complete engagements beats forty tools used once each, every time.

The second mistake is subtler. Handing a client a raw Nessus export is not a penetration test; it is a scan. What the client pays for is the judgement that reduces 400 findings to the five that matter this week, and that judgement is the thing no tool performs for you.

The third one shows up in follow-up questions. If you can fire an SMB exploit from Metasploit but cannot explain what SMB is doing on the wire, Wireshark will teach you more in a weekend than another exploitation tutorial will in a month. That is also why the VAPT and ethical hacking course in Hyderabad we run opens with networking fundamentals and Linux before any exploitation module, rather than starting with the exciting part.
