What Should You Look For in Online VAPT Training?

Check four things before you pay: the number of live instructor hours, whether a practice lab is included or you must build it, whether the trainer is named, and what the provider promises in writing about placement. Across Google’s first page on 29 September 2026, published teaching time for online VAPT training ranged from 14 hours to 120 hours.

Those four checks are not arbitrary. They are the four places where online delivery can quietly go wrong in a way classroom delivery cannot. Hours go missing because a recorded course counts video minutes as “training”. Labs go missing because nobody has to hand you a machine. Trainers go missing because you never meet the person whose name is on the brochure. And placement language goes soft because it is easier to write “100% placement assistance” in a title tag than to explain what assistance means.

The short version: online VAPT training is a good way to learn and a poor way to be sold to. Ask for hours, lab, trainer and outcome in writing before any payment. Every provider below answers at least one of those four questions on its public page — not one answers all four.

How Many Hours Does Online VAPT Training Actually Run?

Published teaching time for online VAPT training in India runs from 14 hours to 120 hours, an 8.6-fold spread under one phrase. Reading every page-one result on 29 September 2026: Udemy lists 14 hours of lectures, MindMajix and GoLogica both list 40 hours, Isha Training Solutions lists 50-plus live hours, and Hacker School lists 120-plus.

Hours are not quality on their own. A well-taught 40 hours beats a padded 120. But hours are the one input you can compare across providers before you have met anyone, and the spread here is too wide to be about teaching style. It is about what the word “training” is being made to cover — recorded video in one case, four evenings a week for ten weeks in another.

What Does Online VAPT Training Cost Per Live Hour?

Cost per live instructor hour is the only number that compares two online VAPT courses fairly, and only two page-one providers publish both figures. On 29 September 2026 Hacker School worked out at roughly ₹208 per live hour and Isha Training Solutions at roughly ₹198, while GoLogica advertised 40 hours and no price at all.

The calculation is simple and worth doing before every enquiry call. Hacker School published ₹25,000 plus GST for 120-plus hours. Isha published ₹9,900, discounted from ₹25,000, for 50-plus hours. MindMajix published $440 for its live 40-hour track and $160 for the self-paced version, which is about $11 per live hour. Does Online VAPT Training Include a Lab?

Often it does not, and no page-one provider states lab provisioning in comparable terms. Hacker School tells students they will “receive instructions from the trainer to set up their own lab”. MindMajix includes a free VAPT instance with one year of LMS access. GoLogica’s page does not mention lab access at all.

In a classroom this question does not arise. You walk in, the machines are there, and if your virtual machine will not boot someone fixes it in front of you. Online, the lab becomes your problem the moment nobody says otherwise. That is not a scandal — building your own lab is a genuinely useful skill and the first module of any serious programme covers it. It only becomes a problem when you discover it in week two, on a laptop with 8 GB of RAM, alone.

There are three honest models, and any of them is fine as long as the provider names which one it uses. The provider hosts a lab you connect to. The provider gives you a guided build on your own machine. Or you subscribe to a third-party platform and the course tells you which rooms to do and when. The failure mode is the fourth option: the page says “real-time labs” and nothing else, and you find out on day one.

Ask: hosted, guided or bring-your-own?

Three legitimate answers. “Real-time labs” is not one of them. Get the answer before you pay, in the same message as the fee.

Ask: for how long after the course ends?

MindMajix publishes one year of LMS access. Most pages publish nothing. Practice does not stop on the last day of class.

Ask: what does my laptop need?

A VAPT lab runs an attacker machine and a target at once. Plan for 16 GB of RAM, an SSD, and virtualisation enabled in BIOS.

Ask: what am I allowed to test?

Only systems you own or have written authorisation for. Any course that is vague about this is teaching you a habit that ends careers.

What Does Your Own Practice Lab Cost?

If the course does not provide a lab, budget for one: TryHackMe’s free tier gives free rooms only, caps the browser AttackBox at one hour a day, and excludes networks and personal hackable instances. Premium was €10.50 a month billed annually when we read the TryHackMe pricing page on 29 September 2026.

One hour a day is the number to sit with. Recon on a single target can eat that before you have opened a scanner. It is enough to follow along with a tutorial and not enough to get stuck, which is where the learning actually happens. The paid tier removes the cap and unlocks the network rooms, which are the ones that resemble an enterprise environment.

None of this is a reason to avoid an online course. It is a reason to add the line to your budget, because a course fee plus twelve months of lab access is the real cost of learning this online, and only one number of the two usually appears on a landing page.

Is There a Free VAPT Course Available?

Yes, free VAPT material exists on YouTube, Udemy’s free previews and TryHackMe’s free rooms, and it is genuinely useful for concepts and tool syntax. What free material cannot give you is a person checking your method, a reviewer marking your report, or an interview. On 29 September 2026 TryHackMe’s free plan capped lab time at one hour a day.

Use the free tier properly and it will save you money rather than replace the course. Spend a month on networking, Linux and the OWASP Top 10 before you enrol anywhere. You will then be able to judge a demo session on its content instead of its confidence, and you will not spend the first four weeks of a paid programme catching up. How Do You Get a VAPT Certification?

There is no exam called VAPT, so you certify through a vendor: EC-Council’s CEH, OffSec’s OSCP, INE’s eJPT or TCM Security’s PNPT. An online course prepares you for one of those and issues its own certificate of completion. EC-Council places CEH Master in a tier expecting more than two years of networking knowledge.

That last detail is worth knowing before you buy a beginner course marketed as CEH preparation. EC-Council’s own VAPT career path sorts its certifications into tiers, and the Core tier that holds CEH Master is labelled “More than 2 years Networking Knowledge”. Its Foundational tier is the one marked “No Experience Required”. The vendor is telling you the order; the marketing around the vendor often is not.

Two costs follow from this. The course fee is one. The exam voucher is another, it is paid to the vendor, and no institute can include it unless it says so explicitly. We compared the four realistic exam routes and what each one suits in our guide to the best VAPT certifications for 2026.

Is VAPT a Good Career in India?

Yes, by volume: Naukri listed 5,984 VAPT vacancies across India on 29 September 2026, with 1,830 of them filed under IT and Information Security. The honest caveat is the entry gate — in Hyderabad and Secunderabad, 534 VAPT listings were open, but only 55 of them sat in the ₹0–3 lakh band.

Read those two numbers together and the market becomes legible. Demand is real and it is not concentrated at the bottom. In Hyderabad the ₹6–10 lakh band held 300 listings and the ₹10–15 lakh band held 255, against 55 in the lowest band. This is a market that pays once you are in and does not have many doors at the entrance, which is exactly why the portfolio and the report matter more here than the certificate.

Can You Actually Work Remotely as a VAPT Analyst in India?

Mostly no, and this is the fact online course pages leave out. Of 5,984 VAPT vacancies on Naukri on 29 September 2026, 5,693 were work from office, 161 hybrid and only 130 remote — 2.2 per cent. In Hyderabad the remote count was six out of 534, or 1.1 per cent.

This is not an argument against learning online. It is an argument against the implied promise. Online training is about how you learn, not where you will work, and a course page that blurs the two is selling you a lifestyle it does not control. Penetration testing touches client data and scoped internal networks, and most Indian employers still want that happening from an office.

What it does change is what you should optimise for. If ninety-eight per cent of the roles are on-site, then proximity to a hiring market matters, and so does whether your training provider has one. That is the practical case for a hybrid route: learn online when it suits your week, use the classroom at KPHB when you want someone looking over your shoulder, and be physically near the employers who are hiring.

Can You Learn Cyber Security in 3 Months?

You can learn one branch of it in three months, not the field. Three months of live online VAPT training at 12 hours a week is about 150 hours, which covers networking, Linux, web technologies, recon, scanning and basic exploitation. Active Directory, cloud, mobile and report writing need the months after that.

The three-month claim is popular because it is nearly true and completely unhelpful. Cyber security is not one subject — VAPT, SOC operations, GRC and identity are different jobs with different daily work. Three focused months will make you competent in the first half of one of them. Pretending otherwise is how people arrive at interviews able to run a scanner and unable to explain a finding.

If defensive work interests you more than offensive, that is a different route with a different market, and we mapped it in the SOC Analyst L1 & L2 programme rather than here.

Which Tools Should an Online VAPT Course Cover?

A credible online VAPT course teaches at least twelve tools inside the modules that use them: Kali Linux, Nmap, Wireshark, Burp Suite, Metasploit, Nessus, OpenVAS, Hashcat, John The Ripper, SQLMap, Gobuster and Amass. Ask where each tool appears in the syllabus, because a tool list without a module map is marketing.

A tool list is the easiest thing in the world to publish and the easiest to fake. The useful question is not “do you teach Burp Suite” but “in which module, after which prerequisite, and what do I produce with it”. Burp Suite belongs after HTTP and web application architecture, not in week one. Metasploit belongs after you can find a service worth exploiting. What Should You Ask Before You Pay?

Ask six questions in the demo: how many live instructor hours, who teaches them, is a lab included and for how long, are sessions recorded, what exactly is promised about placement, and is the total fee inclusive of GST. Get the answers in writing, not in a phone call.
