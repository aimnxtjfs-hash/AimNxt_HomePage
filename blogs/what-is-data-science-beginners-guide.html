Google's first page for this question is eight definition pages. IBM, Amazon Web Services, Coursera, W3Schools, Berkeley, Harvard, GeeksforGeeks and a university blog. They all say roughly the same sentence, and the sentence is correct. The Indian page on that list runs to about 350 words of actual article and carries three headings.

Now change the search to "what is data science 2026" and the page turns over completely. Reddit threads with a hundred-odd comments asking whether the field is still worth entering. A Medium post titled "Data Science in 2026: Prepare to Be Uncomfortable". Towards Data Science asking "Is It Still Worth It?". Google knows what people are really asking. The definition pages are answering a different question.

So this guide does both. The definition first, because you asked for it and it should be short. Then the part nobody on that first page publishes: which of these jobs will actually hire you, and how narrow the door is. Every job figure below was read from naukri.com for Hyderabad and Secunderabad on 14 October 2026, and we have put the uncomfortable numbers in rather than leave them out.

What is data science?

Data science is the practice of using statistics, programming and domain knowledge to turn raw data into decisions a business can act on. The US Bureau of Labor Statistics puts it more plainly: data scientists use analytical tools and techniques to extract meaningful insights from data. In Hyderabad, 3,732 live adverts currently carry that job title.

Two words in that definition do the real work. "Decisions", because an analysis nobody acts on is a hobby. And "domain knowledge", because a model of hospital readmissions built by somebody who has never spoken to a doctor will be technically clean and practically useless. The statistics and the programming are the part a course can teach you in a few months. The judgement takes longer.

What data science is not: a single job title you apply for after a three-month course. That is the misreading this page exists to correct, and the numbers further down show exactly how expensive the misreading is in Hyderabad.

The whole guide in four lines

Data science turns data into decisions. It is hired as four separate jobs, not one. In Hyderabad the four jobs have almost identical advert volume but the fresher door varies by 4.4 times, and it runs backwards from what the brochures imply. Start with the job that is actually hiring freshers, then move across.

What is data science in simple words?

In simple words, data science is asking a question, finding the data that answers it, cleaning that data until it is usable, building a model that predicts something, then explaining the result to somebody who is not technical. The last step decides whether you keep the job. Most beginners rehearse only the model-building step.

Here is the version a trainer uses in a first class. A retail chain wants to know which customers are about to stop buying. Somebody has to decide what "about to stop" even means in months. Somebody has to pull two years of transactions out of a database that spells the same city four different ways. Somebody has to notice that half the rows are a pricing test and throw them out. Only then does anyone fit a model.

That first half is where the time goes, and it is why Python and SQL sit at the front of every honest syllabus rather than at the end.

What are the steps in a real data science project?

A data science project runs through six steps: frame the question, collect the data, clean it, explore it, model it, then communicate and monitor the result. Cleaning and exploring take most of the calendar. The AimNxt Data Science curriculum follows that order too, opening with Python and SQL and statistics before any machine learning module.

The step everybody forgets is the last one. A model that was accurate in March is often wrong by September because the customers changed, not because the maths broke. Monitoring and retraining is an ordinary part of the job, and it is the reason employers ask for engineering habits — version control, repeatable scripts, documented assumptions — alongside statistics.

1

Frame the question

Turn "why are sales down" into something measurable with a date range, a definition and a success test. Get this wrong and everything after it is wasted.

2

Collect the data

Usually SQL against a warehouse, sometimes an API or a file somebody emails you. This is why SQL is the most-advertised skill in the whole field.

3

Clean it

Missing values, duplicate rows, four spellings of one city, timestamps in two time zones. The least glamorous step and reliably the longest.

4

Explore it

Exploratory data analysis. Distributions, correlations, outliers. Half the time the answer is here and no model is needed at all.

5

Model it

Regression, classification, clustering, or a neural network if the problem genuinely calls for one. The step courses spend the most time selling.

6

Communicate and monitor

One chart and three sentences a manager can act on, then watch the model as the world shifts under it. This is the step that gets people promoted.

What exactly does a data scientist do?

A data scientist decides which data is relevant, gathers and cleans it, builds and maintains models including machine learning models, presents findings to technical and non-technical audiences, and advises on business decisions. Those five duties are the US Bureau of Labor Statistics list, and Hyderabad adverts ask for the same five, in a different order.

What the list hides is the split of the week. In most Indian product and services teams a data scientist spends more time in SQL, spreadsheets and meetings than in a modelling notebook. The modelling is the interesting fifteen per cent. Candidates who have only ever practised the interesting fifteen per cent interview badly, because the first screening round is almost always SQL.

Is data science one job, or four?

Data science is sold as one job and hired as four: data analyst, data engineer, data scientist and machine learning engineer. In Hyderabad and Secunderabad on 14 October 2026 the four titles returned 3,249, 4,118, 3,732 and 3,617 live adverts on naukri.com — within 27 per cent of each other, so no single title dominates.

That is the first thing a beginner needs and nobody on the first page of Google says it. The four jobs sit on the same project at different points. The data engineer builds the pipes that move the data. The analyst answers questions about what already happened. The data scientist builds the thing that predicts what happens next. The machine learning engineer puts that prediction into a live product and keeps it running.

They need overlapping but genuinely different skills, they are interviewed differently, and they do not pay the same. Treating them as one career is how people spend eight months on deep learning for a role whose first interview round is a window function. We walk through the two closest of the four in detail in our guide on data analyst vs data scientist.

What is the difference between data science and data analytics?

Data analytics explains what already happened; data science predicts what happens next. An analyst answers "which products lost margin last quarter" with SQL and a dashboard. A data scientist answers "which customers will churn next quarter" with a trained model. On AmbitionBox the data analyst designation carries about 1.5 lakh reported salaries against 61,500 for data scientist.

That ratio matters more than the definitions. On the same salary site, roughly 2.4 times as many people in India report their job as data analyst. It is the larger population, the wider fresher door and the shorter runway, which is why the honest recommendation for most beginners in Hyderabad is to enter as an analyst and move across once you have production data experience and somebody paying you to learn.

Nothing about that path is a downgrade. Almost every data scientist we train in Kukatpally arrived from an analyst, support or developer seat. What skills do you actually need for data science?

Five: Python, SQL, statistics, one visualisation tool, and the ability to explain a result in three sentences. AmbitionBox lists Python, SQL, machine learning, deep learning and artificial intelligence as the top reported skills for the data scientist designation. SQL comes first in interviews even though it comes last in the marketing.

Take them in that order and the reason becomes obvious. SQL is how you get the data, so the screening round tests it. Python is how you shape and model the data. Statistics is how you know whether the result means anything, and it is the skill most self-taught candidates skip and most interviewers probe. A visualisation tool is how the answer reaches a decision-maker. The explanation is what gets you hired over somebody with better accuracy scores.

What is not on the list: a sixth framework. Candidates arrive with TensorFlow on the CV and no window functions, and the interview ends in the first twenty minutes. Depth in the first three beats breadth across ten.

The five, with the honest test for each

SQL — can you write a query with a join, a group-by, a subquery and a window function without looking it up? That is the screening round, in Hyderabad and everywhere else.
Python — not syntax quizzes. Can you load a messy CSV, fix the dates, handle the missing values and produce a clean table somebody else could reuse?
Statistics — can you say what a p-value is for, why correlation is not causation, and when a model is overfitting? Most candidates fail the third one.
Visualisation — one tool taught properly. Can you build a chart that answers the question rather than displaying all the data you have?
Explanation — three sentences, no jargon, for a manager who will not read your notebook. Practise it out loud, because it is the hardest one to fake.

Is data science difficult to learn?

Data science is not conceptually difficult, but it is unusually wide, and the width is what defeats people. You are learning a programming language, a query language, applied statistics, a visualisation tool and a communication skill at once. Most people who drop out stop in the statistics block, not the machine learning block.

The genuinely hard parts, in order: statistics that you have to reason with rather than recite, debugging data rather than code, and accepting that a well-built model can still be the wrong answer to the business question. None of that needs a mathematics degree. It needs a few months of consistent, corrected practice, which is a different thing from a few months of watching videos.

The practical test is simple. If you can sit with a messy spreadsheet for two hours and feel curious rather than irritated, you will be fine. If that sounds unbearable, the field will be a grind regardless of how good the course is.

Which is better, computer science or data science?

For a first degree, computer science is the safer choice; data science is the better specialisation on top of it. Hyderabad's own adverts make the case: 1,939 of 3,732 data scientist listings sit in the Engineering, Software and QA department, and 1,612 fall under a software development role category.

Read that honestly and it says employers largely treat data science as a software engineering specialism. A computer science graduate can move into data science; the reverse move, from a narrow data science degree into general software engineering, is harder because the fundamentals were traded away for tooling. If you already hold a data science degree this is not bad news, but it does mean treating programming as a first-class skill rather than a support one.

For working professionals the question usually does not arise. You have the degree you have, and what decides the outcome is whether you can show three projects and pass a SQL round.

Do you need a master's degree to get into data science?

Not formally, but in Hyderabad the adverts lean postgraduate. The US Bureau of Labor Statistics lists a bachelor's degree as the typical entry-level education for data scientists, noting some roles require a master's or doctorate. Yet 1,902 of 3,732 Hyderabad adverts — 51 per cent — filter to Any Postgraduate, against 664 naming B.Tech or B.E.

That is a gate worth seeing before you plan around it. It is not absolute: Any Graduate still matched 3,022 of the same adverts, so the postgraduate filter is a preference in the listing rather than a hard bar in most cases. But it does explain why freshers with only a bachelor's find the data scientist title harder to enter than the job description suggests, and it is another argument for entering at analyst level and converting later.

One live example from the fresher set makes the point without us editorialising. Among the 59 listings open to zero years was a data engineering fresher post stating it would consider candidates from IITs and NITs only. Filters like that exist. Knowing they exist is more useful than being surprised by them.

Is it worth becoming a data scientist in 2026?

Yes, if you enter through a door that is actually open and expect a two-to-three-year run-up rather than a six-month one. The field is not shrinking — Hyderabad carries 14,716 live adverts across the four data titles — but the fresher entry point has narrowed to 1.6 per cent for the data scientist title specifically.

The anxiety on Reddit is not irrational, and it is worth naming rather than dismissing. Large language models have absorbed a chunk of the routine work: boilerplate code, first-pass exploratory analysis, chart generation. What they have not absorbed is deciding which question is worth asking, knowing whether a result is trustworthy, and being accountable for a decision. That is the part that was always the job.

What has genuinely changed is the entry level. The tasks a fresher used to be hired to do are the tasks most easily automated, which is one plain explanation for why the fresher door is narrow. The practical response is to enter where hiring still happens, build visible production experience, and keep moving. It is also why a syllabus that reaches large language models and prompt engineering, as the AimNxt module list does, is more useful in 2026 than one that stops at regression.
